**Note on sourcing method:** In this research session, direct WebFetch access to eur-lex.europa.eu and to most law-firm/industry sites (Sidley, Skadden, Osborne Clarke, EY, AO Shearman, Baker McKenzie, Bird & Bird, Noerr, Wikipedia, streamlex.eu, ehds-jurist.nl, ictrecht.nl, etc.) was blocked by the network egress proxy in this environment. ## What is the exact date the Regulation entered into force, and what are the "general application" dates (2027/2029/2031)?

### Takeaway
Regulation (EU) 2025/327 was published in the EU Official Journal on 5 March 2025 and entered into force 20 days later; sources are split between 25 and 26 March 2025 for the exact entry-into-force date, but there is strong consensus that it will not generally apply until **26 March 2027**, with major further tranches of obligations applying on **26 March 2029**, **26 March 2031**, and a final tranche on **26 March 2035**.

### Cited Findings
- The Regulation was published in the Official Journal of the EU on 5 March 2025. — [Chambers and Partners](https://chambers.com/legal-trends/update-on-the-european-health-data-space); [Baker McKenzie](https://healthcarelifesciences.bakermckenzie.com/2025/03/05/european-health-data-space-regulation-published-whats-next/)
- Most sources state the Regulation "came into force on 26 March 2025," beginning the transition period toward application. — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/3347); [Kennedys Law](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/the-european-health-data-space-is-in-force-implications-for-healthcare-medtech-and-life-sciences/)
- At least one source states entry into force was "25 March 2025, 20 days after publication in the EU Official Journal" — a discrepancy with the more common "26 March 2025" figure found across most other sources (WebSearch synthesis, exact originating source not individually identified).
- General/default application date: **26 March 2027** ("the EHDS will not apply until March 26, 2027," to give the Commission time to adopt implementing acts). — [StreamLex Art. 105 summary via WebSearch](https://streamlex.eu/articles/ehds-en-art-105/); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/3347)
- Quoted/paraphrased text attributed to **Article 105 (Entry into force and application)**: "Articles 3 to 15, Article 23(2) to (6), Articles 25, 26, 27, 47, 48 and 49 shall apply from 26 March 2029 to priority categories of personal electronic health data referred to in Article 14(1), points (a), (b) and (c), and to EHR systems intended by the manufacturer to process such categories of data; and from 26 March 2031 to priority categories of personal electronic health data referred to in Article 14(1), points (d), (e) and (f), and to EHR systems intended by the manufacturer to process such categories of data." — [StreamLex Art. 105 via WebSearch synthesis](https://streamlex.eu/articles/ehds-en-art-105/)
- Chapter IV (secondary use) is stated to apply generally from **26 March 2029**. — [WebSearch synthesis citing Art. 105](https://streamlex.eu/articles/ehds-en-art-105/)
- Certain Chapter IV governance/infrastructure provisions apply earlier, from **26 March 2027**: "Article 55(6), Article 70, Article 73(5), Article 75(1) and (12), Article 77(4) and Article 78(6)" — covering templates for health data access applications/permits/requests, secure processing environment requirements, certain HealthData@EU provisions, dataset descriptions, and the data quality and utility label. — [WebSearch synthesis citing Art. 105](https://streamlex.eu/articles/ehds-en-art-105/)
- Article 75(12) (a HealthData@EU-related provision) applies from 26 March 2027, but "implementing acts referred to in Article 75(12) shall apply from 26 March 2029." — [WebSearch synthesis citing Art. 75](https://streamlex.eu/articles/ehds-en-art-75/)
- Article 75(5), concerning the ability of non-EU/third countries and international organisations to become "authorised participants" in HealthData@EU, applies **ten years after entry into force, i.e. from 26 March 2035**. — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/28012-european-health-data-space-ehds-regulation-2025327-came-into-force-with-grace-period)
- A dedicated Dutch legal-timeline resource ("When does the EHDS apply? Timeline 2025–2035 per obligation") frames the milestones as: 2025 entry into force; by 26 March 2027 Member States/Commission must complete preparatory steps (designating supervisory authorities for primary and secondary use; Commission adopting secondary legislation on ICT system requirements); 26 March 2029 Priority Category 1 data (Patient Summary, ePrescription, eDispensation) plus Chapter IV (secondary use) become mandatory, and HDABs must be operational; 26 March 2031 Priority Category 2 (imaging, labs, discharge reports) and "Chapter III (EHR systems in service)" become mandatory; 26 March 2035 final transitional provisions complete. — [ehds-jurist.nl timeline, via WebSearch](https://ehds-jurist.nl/en/tijdlijn)

### Inferences
- The EHDS follows a four-stage rollout keyed to entry-into-force-plus-N-years: +2 years (2027, general application/governance groundwork), +4 years (2029, first data-category tranche + most secondary-use rules), +6 years (2031, second data-category tranche + remaining secondary-use categories), +10 years (2035, narrow provision on third-country/international-organisation participation in HealthData@EU).
- The "2027/2029/2031" figures commonly cited in press coverage refer to this +2/+4/+6-year cadence from the March 2025 entry into force, confirming the request's framing is correct, with the addition of a further 2035 milestone for one specific provision (Article 75(5)) that is less commonly reported.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the precise entry-into-force date (25 vs 26 March 2025) against the primary EUR-Lex text due to WebFetch access being blocked; this should be confirmed directly from the consolidated regulation before publication.
- I could not obtain the full, verbatim text of Article 105 (only search-engine-synthesized paraphrases/quotations of it), so exact sub-paragraph numbering and complete list of articles per date tranche should be verified against the official text.

## Primary use: cross-border exchange deadlines by data category, and any "sending" vs "receiving" distinction

### Takeaway
Cross-border exchange of health data under MyHealth@EU is staggered by data category: **Patient Summaries and ePrescriptions/eDispensations by 26 March 2029**, and **medical images/imaging reports, lab results, and hospital discharge reports by 26 March 2031**. I found no sourced evidence of a distinct "sending" vs "receiving" deadline split — this appears to be a gap in available reporting rather than a confirmed feature of the Regulation.

### Cited Findings
- "26 March 2029: ... for primary use, the exchange of the first group of priority categories of health data (Patient Summaries, ePrescriptions/eDispensations) in all EU Member States." — [Digital Policy Alert, via WebSearch](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/3347)
- "26 March 2031: For primary use, the exchange of the second group of priority categories of health data (medical images, lab results, and hospital discharge reports) should be operational in all EU Member States." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://www.ey.com/en_gr/technical/tax/tax-alerts/regulation-2025-327-establishing-ehds) (cross-confirmed by [Uroweb](https://uroweb.org/the-european-health-data-space-regulation-ehds) and [saludcomply.com](https://saludcomply.com/en/ehds/) in the same search result set)
- "On 26 March 2029, the first set of cross-border functionalities is supposed to be available. These include access to a summary of a patient's medical history, and the requirements will expand in 2031 to include medical images and related reports, laboratory and other diagnostic results, and hospital discharge reports." MyHealth@EU "moves from a voluntary European project to a mandatory participation for all Member States, with the service perimeter progressively broadened." — [noze.it, via WebSearch](https://www.noze.it/en/insights/ehds-regulation-2025-327/)
- "Under the EHDS timeline, the first group of priority data categories — patient summaries and ePrescriptions — must be exchangeable across all 27 member states via MyHealth@EU by March 2029." — [WebSearch synthesis of 2026 rollout coverage](https://www.globsec.org/what-we-do/commentaries/running-some-us-can-walk-implementing-european-health-data-space-ehds)
- The European Commission adopted **Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2083 on MyHealth@EU** (adopted 18 September 2026, published in the OJ 21 September 2026), described as laying down rules for the central interoperability platform for cross-border exchange of personal electronic health data. — [Die Produktkanzlei, via WebSearch](https://www.produktkanzlei.com/en/2026/09/23/myhealtheu-and-healthdcat-ap/); [EU Law Live](https://eulawlive.com/oj-commission-adopts-implementing-regulation-on-myhealtheu-cross-border-health-data-platform/)

### Inferences
- The "Priority Category 1" (Patient Summary, ePrescription, eDispensation) vs "Priority Category 2" (medical imaging/reports, lab results, discharge reports) split maps directly onto Article 14(1) points (a)-(c) vs (d)-(f) as referenced in the Article 105 application-date provisions, and onto the 2029 vs 2031 deadlines respectively.
- Because a MyHealth@EU implementing regulation was only adopted in September 2026 (over a year after entry into force and roughly 2.5 years ahead of the 2029 deadline), the technical/operational build-out for cross-border exchange was still being finalized as of the most recent findings in this research (September 2026), which is consistent with the phased timeline but suggests the operational runway before the 2029 deadline is compressing.

### Gaps
- No source found in this research distinguishes a separate "sending" obligation deadline from a "receiving" obligation deadline for Member States under MyHealth@EU. It is possible the Regulation does not create such a formal distinction, or that it exists in the text but was not surfaced by the searches conducted. This should be checked directly against Articles 3–15 and Article 23 of the consolidated text.
- No detail was found on any additional "other categories" of primary-use data beyond the two priority groups (e.g., a possible future/optional third category), or on any delegated-act mechanism to expand Annex I categories over time (see the delegated-acts section below, which does reference a mechanism to amend Annex I).

## Secondary use: HDAB establishment/operational deadlines and data-holder response obligations

### Takeaway
Member States must have designated their Health Data Access Bodies (HDABs) and related supervisory structures by around **26 March 2027**, with HDABs required to be fully operational by **26 March 2029**, when most secondary-use rules (Chapter IV) become applicable; data holders must generally respond to/fulfil health data access requests **within three months** (extendable in exceptional circumstances), and must submit dataset descriptions starting **26 March 2029**.

### Cited Findings
- "By March 2027, Member States must designate Health Data Access Bodies (HDABs)." "HDABs will be fully operational by March 2029 to facilitate regulated access to health data for secondary use." — [WebSearch synthesis of multiple sources including GA4GH policy brief and health.ec.europa.eu FAQ](https://www.ga4gh.org/news_item/the-european-health-data-space-from-approval-to-national-implementation/)
- "By 26 March 2027 at the latest, Member States and the European Commission must have completed various preparatory steps. Member States must designate supervisory authorities for primary and secondary use." — [ehds-jurist.nl timeline, via WebSearch](https://ehds-jurist.nl/en/tijdlijn)
- "Data holders are required to make electronic health data available upon request to health data access bodies within a reasonable timeframe, no later than three months from receiving the request." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://www.insideprivacy.com/big-data/ehds-series-2-the-european-health-data-space-from-the-health-data-holders-perspective/) (cross-referenced against [EPF analysis PDF](https://www.eu-patient.eu/globalassets/ehds-analysis---final.pdf))
- "EHD must be made available to the HDAB within a maximum time limit of three months, though in exceptional circumstances, a longer time limit may be obtained." "Dataset descriptions must be submitted starting from 26 March 2029." "As of 26 March 2029, data access requests can be made at any time, however, for some data categories, such requests can only be made as of 26 March 2031." — [Bird & Bird BioTalk, via WebSearch](https://biotalk.twobirds.com/post/102k70n/health-data-holders-obligations-under-the-ehds-what-when-and-how-will-we-know)
- Rules on secondary use "will also start to apply for most data categories (e.g. data from electronic health records)" from 26 March 2029, and "for the remaining data categories (e.g. genomic data)" from 26 March 2031. — [WebSearch synthesis](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/3347)
- "Provisions on secondary use of health data (except for specific data such as human genetic, molecular, or clinical trials data) will apply four years after the EHDS Regulation becomes law" (26 March 2029); "provisions on secondary use of previously excluded data types (such as genetic, molecular, or clinical trials data) will apply six years after" (26 March 2031). — [Arnold & Porter, via WebSearch](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/03/european-health-data-space-regulation-published)
- The **HealthDCAT-AP Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2098** (implementing Article 77(4) of the EHDS Regulation) specifies minimum metadata elements data holders must provide for dataset descriptions for secondary use; it enters into force 11 October 2026 and "applies as of March 26, 2029." — [Die Produktkanzlei, via WebSearch](https://www.produktkanzlei.com/en/2026/09/23/myhealtheu-and-healthdcat-ap/)
- Microenterprises (fewer than 10 employees, turnover/balance sheet under €2 million) are generally exempt from health-data-holder obligations, though "Member States will be able to override this exemption according to their national law." An enterprise that changes size category with respect to Chapter IV obligations "should immediately communicate with [the] pertinent HDAB with the aim of finding a suitable solution." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://www.akd.eu/insights/european-health-data-space-regulation-cross-border-access-to-health-data)

### Inferences
- The secondary-use track runs on the same 2027/2029/2031 cadence as primary use: 2027 for institutional/legal groundwork (HDAB designation, governance templates), 2029 for full operability and most data categories, 2031 for sensitive/harder categories (genomic, molecular, clinical trial data).
- The near-simultaneous 2026 adoption of the HealthDCAT-AP and MyHealth@EU implementing regulations (both keyed to 2029 application) indicates the Commission is sequencing its secondary legislation to land shortly before the 2029 deadline, consistent with, but very close to, the statutory 26 March 2027 deadline for the Commission's own preparatory implementing acts (see delegated/implementing acts section).

### Gaps
- I found no explicit extended deadline (e.g., extra months) specifically for micro/small data holders' three-month response obligation; sources only describe the general exemption of microenterprises from data-holder status, not a graduated timeline for small-but-not-micro enterprises. This should be checked directly against Chapter IV (secondary use) provisions, in particular any article defining data holder categories.
- Could not confirm whether "Member States must designate HDABs by 2027" is a hard statutory deadline actually appearing in Article 105, or an inference/summary made by secondary sources from the general 2027 application date plus governance-article list (Article 55(6), 70, 73(5), 75(1)/(12), 77(4), 78(6)). Recommend verifying against the primary text.

## EHR system conformity/self-certification timeline and grandfathering

### Takeaway
EHR system manufacturers face a mandatory conformity self-assessment/CE-marking regime under EHDS Chapter II (placing on the market) and Chapter III (systems in service), with the essential requirements (Annex II) becoming applicable on the same 2029/2031 category-based schedule as primary use — 2029 for systems intended to process Priority Category 1 data (patient summaries, e-prescriptions, e-dispensations) and 2031 for Priority Category 2 (imaging, lab results, discharge reports) — and the Commission was required to adopt implementing acts on common specifications for these essential requirements by 26 March 2027. No explicit grandfathering/transition provision for systems already on the market was confirmed in available sources.

### Cited Findings
- "The EHDS establishes a mandatory conformity self-assessment scheme for EHR systems processing priority categories of personal electronic health data... EHR systems are required to undergo a certification process that demonstrates their conformity with standards, including self-certification for interoperability and security... manufacturers to demonstrate compliance with essential requirements – laid down in Annex II of the regulation – before placing systems on the EU market." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eu-establishes-mandatory-ce-marking-electronic-health-record-systems)
- "The rules will first apply from 26 March 2029 to EHR systems covering patient summaries, electronic prescriptions, and electronic dispensations. The requirements will then be extended from 26 March 2031 to EHR systems covering medical imaging studies, medical test results, and discharge reports." — [Timelex, via WebSearch](https://www.timelex.eu/en/blog/navigating-ehds-understanding-electronic-health-record-systems-requirements)
- "Central to this framework are two harmonised software components... the interoperability component... using the European Electronic Health Record Exchange Format (EEHRxF)... and the logging component... Annex II to the EHDS Regulation sets out the essential requirements for harmonised software components of EHR systems." "The detailed technical specifications applicable to these harmonised software components are to be established by the European Commission through implementing acts in the form of common specifications, which are expected to be adopted by 26 March 2027." — [WebSearch synthesis citing PMC article and StreamLex Annex II](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/ehds-en-annex-ii/)
- "By 26 March 2027, the Commission shall, by means of implementing acts, adopt common specifications in respect of the essential requirements laid down in Annex II, including a common template and a time limit for implementing those common specifications." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://streamlex.eu/articles/ehds-en-art-36/)
- The dedicated Dutch timeline resource frames 26 March 2031 as when "Chapter III (EHR systems in service)" becomes mandatory, alongside Priority Category 2 data obligations. — [ehds-jurist.nl, via WebSearch](https://ehds-jurist.nl/en/tijdlijn)
- "The Chapter focusing on EHR systems will apply six years after entry into force, which means the EHR systems requirements take effect around March 2031... Market-readiness challenges are identified, particularly for SMEs and start-ups. The generous grace period reflects the intensity of effort that will be required..." However, this same search result explicitly noted it could not find "specific information about grandfathering provisions for existing EHR systems already in the market during the transition period." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://healthcarelifesciences.bakermckenzie.com/2024/11/04/ehds-deep-dive-ehr-systems-wellness-apps-and-medical-devices-part-4/)

### Inferences
- There appear to be two distinct EHR-related chapters with different scope: Chapter II (EHR systems being placed on the market — new systems, subject to CE marking/self-certification) and Chapter III (EHR systems already in service), with Chapter III specifically flagged as applying from 26 March 2031 per the Dutch timeline source. This suggests the Regulation itself may build in a de facto transition/grace period for legacy ("in service") systems by giving them until the later 2031 date (and possibly only for the Priority Category 2 functional scope), rather than requiring immediate retrofitting — but this reading was not corroborated by a source explicitly describing it as "grandfathering."
- The 26 March 2027 deadline for the Commission's common-specifications implementing acts (Annex II) is a critical bottleneck date: manufacturers cannot finalize conformity assessments until these specifications exist, which compresses the effective preparation window between 2027 and the 2029/2031 application dates.

### Gaps
- No source explicitly confirmed or denied a formal "grandfathering" clause exempting EHR systems already placed on the market before the relevant application date from retroactive compliance. This is a specific, high-value question that likely requires direct reading of Chapter II/III articles (placing on the market vs. systems in service) in the consolidated EUR-Lex text, which I could not access due to the network block in this environment.
- Could not confirm exact article numbers for the Chapter II/Chapter III boundary or the precise CE-marking/self-certification procedural articles beyond the general references to "Annex II" and "Article 36 (common specifications)."

## Different deadlines for large/medium vs micro/small enterprises

### Takeaway
The clearest enterprise-size distinction found is a categorical **exemption** (not merely a delayed deadline) for microenterprises (fewer than 10 employees, turnover/balance sheet under €2 million) from health-data-holder obligations under Chapter IV, subject to Member States' ability to override this nationally; no separate delayed compliance deadline (e.g., extra months) specifically for small (non-micro) or medium enterprises was found.

### Cited Findings
- "The EHDS regulation exempts natural persons, including individual researchers and legal persons that qualify as microenterprises, from the obligations on health data holders. Microenterprises are defined as enterprises employing fewer than 10 people and with an annual turnover or balance sheet of less than €2 million. However, Member States will be able to override this exemption according to their national law." — [AKD, via WebSearch](https://www.akd.eu/insights/european-health-data-space-regulation-cross-border-access-to-health-data)
- "An enterprise that changes its category with respect to obligations set out by Chapter IV of the EHDS regulation should immediately communicate with pertinent HDAB with the aim of finding a suitable solution regarding their health data availability for secondary use." — [WebSearch synthesis, same source set](https://www.akd.eu/insights/european-health-data-space-regulation-cross-border-access-to-health-data)
- "Market-readiness challenges are identified, particularly for SMEs and start-ups. The generous grace period reflects the intensity of effort that will be required in order to set up the necessary digital infrastructure..." (general statement about SME challenges, not a distinct legal deadline). — [WebSearch synthesis](https://healthcarelifesciences.bakermckenzie.com/2024/11/04/ehds-deep-dive-ehr-systems-wellness-apps-and-medical-devices-part-4/)
- The EHDS Regulation is described as requiring "concrete action, capacity-building and training to support the digital health community, particularly public authorities, healthcare providers, and service providers, especially Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs), in the deployment of digital health services and systems" — a support/capacity-building commitment rather than a deadline extension. (WebSearch synthesis, source not individually pinpointed in results)

### Inferences
- The Regulation's SME accommodation appears to be structured primarily as a scope exemption (microenterprises excluded from being "data holders" at all) rather than as a staggered compliance timetable for larger vs. smaller entities. This differs from the pattern in some other EU digital/data regulations (e.g., AI Act) that use explicit extra-time provisions for SMEs.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence was found of a specific extra-time deadline for small (but not micro) enterprises acting as EHR system manufacturers or as data holders. This should be verified directly against Chapter II (EHR manufacturer obligations) and Chapter IV (data holder definitions) in the consolidated text, since search-engine synthesis may have missed a specific SME transition article.

## Commission implementing/delegated acts and common specifications still due, with statutory deadlines

### Takeaway
The Regulation requires numerous Commission implementing and delegated acts; the headline statutory deadline found is **26 March 2027** for common specifications on EHR essential requirements (Annex II) via implementing acts. As of the most recent findings (September 2026), the Commission had begun adopting implementing acts, including the EHDS Board implementing regulation (April 2026) and the MyHealth@EU and HealthDCAT-AP implementing regulations (September/October 2026) — all ahead of, but not far ahead of, their respective application dates.

### Cited Findings
- "By 26 March 2027, the Commission shall, by means of implementing acts, adopt common specifications in respect of the essential requirements laid down in Annex II, including a common template and a time limit for implementing those common specifications." — [WebSearch synthesis citing Article 36](https://streamlex.eu/articles/ehds-en-art-36/)
- "Technical details will be set out in delegated and implementing acts – ranging from the technical specifications of the European electronic health record exchange format and detailed requirements for EHR systems manufacturers when they register their systems, to the security measures for secure processing environments." "The common specifications shall take into account the specificities of medical devices and high-risk AI systems, including the state-of-the-art standards for health informatics and the European electronic health record exchange format." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://www.noze.it/en/insights/ehds-regulation-2025-327/)
- "The Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts to amend the Regulation by amending Annex I through the addition, modification or removal of the main characteristics of the priority categories of personal electronic health data, provided that the amendments are aimed at adapting the priority categories of personal electronic health data to technical developments and international standards." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-making-process/adopting-eu-law/implementing-and-delegated-acts_en) (specific article number not confirmed)
- "With the adoption of the first Implementing Act in April 2026, which established the EHDS Board, the implementation marathon has truly begun." — [GLOBSEC commentary, via WebSearch](https://www.globsec.org/what-we-do/commentaries/running-some-us-can-walk-implementing-european-health-data-space-ehds)
- **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/771** (adopted 7 April 2026) "lays down the necessary rules for the establishment, the management and the functioning of the European Health Data Space Board (the 'EHDS Board')" and is "the first implementing act adopted in relation to the EHDS framework." — [EUR-Lex listing / omnilex summary, via WebSearch](https://app.omnilex.ai/en/sources/legislation/eu-legislation--32026r0771/commission-implementing-regulation-eu-2026771-of-7-april-2026-laying-down-the-necessary-measures-for)
- **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2083** on MyHealth@EU was adopted 18 September 2026 and published in the OJ on 21 September 2026. — [Die Produktkanzlei / EU Law Live, via WebSearch](https://www.produktkanzlei.com/en/2026/09/23/myhealtheu-and-healthdcat-ap/)
- **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2098** on HealthDCAT-AP (implementing Article 77(4)) enters into force 11 October 2026 and applies as of 26 March 2029. — [Die Produktkanzlei, via WebSearch](https://www.produktkanzlei.com/en/2026/09/23/myhealtheu-and-healthdcat-ap/)
- A Bitkom (German digital industry association) statement references an upcoming/in-progress "Implementing Act EHDS: Identity Management" as of 2026, indicating further implementing acts (beyond the Board, MyHealth@EU, and HealthDCAT-AP regulations) are still being drafted/adopted. — [Bitkom, via WebSearch](https://www.bitkom.org/EN/List-and-detailpages/Publications/Implementing-Act-EHDS-Identity-Management)
- Separately, "negotiations on its technical elaboration are still ongoing. This includes, for example, the specific exchange formats for electronic health data. Work is currently ongoing on a legislative proposal, with the intention that it will be opened for public consultation in the first half of 2026" — referring to further technical/implementing work in progress as of early 2026. — [WebSearch synthesis, apparently drawing on ictrecht.nl commentary](https://www.ictrecht.nl/en/blog/de-implementatie-van-de-ehds-waar-staan-we-begin-2026)

### Inferences
- The Commission appears to be sequencing implementing acts in a logical dependency order: governance body (EHDS Board, April 2026) first, then core infrastructure regulations (MyHealth@EU, HealthDCAT-AP, September/October 2026), with further acts (e.g., identity management, EEHRxF technical specifications, secure processing environment security measures, Annex II common specifications) still pending as of the most recent available information (September 2026).
- Given the common-specifications deadline for Annex II was statutorily set at 26 March 2027, and as of September 2026 several other implementing acts (identity management, presumably common specifications) were still in progress, there is a plausible risk of the Commission needing to compress its remaining rulemaking work into the final six months before the 2027 deadline — though no source explicitly declared a missed or at-risk deadline.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain a definitive, complete list of all outstanding implementing/delegated acts and their individual statutory deadlines (the Regulation is understood to require numerous such acts across governance, technical specifications, secure processing environments, and EEHRxF format). A full enumeration would require direct review of the consolidated Regulation's empowerment articles (a task better suited to primary-source access than search-engine synthesis).
- Could not confirm the specific article number empowering the Annex I amendment delegated acts, nor its own deadline (if any) for first use.

## Indications of delays, extensions, or acceleration (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
As of the most recent findings (September 2026), the EHDS implementation is proceeding roughly on its statutory schedule rather than showing a formally announced delay or acceleration, but commentary highlights (a) significant unevenness in Member State readiness that creates practical delay risk for the 2029/2031 deadlines, and (b) the Commission's implementing-act work (EHDS Board, MyHealth@EU, HealthDCAT-AP, identity management) landing only in 2026, i.e., roughly a year after entry into force and about a year ahead of the 2027 general-application deadline, which is characterized by at least one commentator as a "marathon" now underway rather than a smooth early start.

### Cited Findings
- Commentary titled "Running Before Some of Us Can Walk: Implementing the European Health Data Space (EHDS) in a Fragmented Union" — framing device suggesting concern about uneven Member State readiness undermining the phased timeline. — [GLOBSEC, via WebSearch](https://www.globsec.org/what-we-do/commentaries/running-some-us-can-walk-implementing-european-health-data-space-ehds)
- "There is significant variation in preparedness among member states. Several Member States, including Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, and Slovakia, show lower eHealth maturity, limited interoperability and governance frameworks, while countries such as Finland and Estonia enter the EHDS era with mature digital health ecosystems, national electronic health records, strong interoperability governance and years of experience with cross-border data exchange." — [WebSearch synthesis, apparently drawing on the GLOBSEC commentary](https://www.globsec.org/what-we-do/commentaries/running-some-us-can-walk-implementing-european-health-data-space-ehds)
- "With the adoption of the first Implementing Act in April 2026, which established the EHDS Board, the implementation marathon has truly begun." — [same GLOBSEC source, via WebSearch]
- "Despite the EHDS having already entered into force, negotiations on its technical elaboration are still ongoing... ### Inferences
- The overall statutory timeline (2027/2029/2031/2035) does not appear to have been formally changed as of September 2026; what commentary flags instead is implementation/readiness risk — i.e., whether all 27 Member States and the healthcare/HIS-vendor ecosystem will in practice be ready by the fixed statutory dates, particularly given uneven starting points in eHealth maturity.
- The relatively late (2026) adoption of foundational implementing acts (EHDS Board, MyHealth@EU, HealthDCAT-AP) relative to the 2025 entry into force suggests the "preparatory" 2025–2027 window is being used close to fully, increasing execution risk for stakeholders (including HIS/EMR vendors and healthcare providers) who need finalized technical specifications well before the 2029 deadline.
