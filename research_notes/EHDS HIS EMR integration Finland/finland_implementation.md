# Finland's EHDS Readiness: National Infrastructure, Authorities, and HIS/EMR Implications

## What is Kanta, who operates it, what services does it provide, and how mature is Finland's national EHR interoperability vs EU peers?

### Takeaway
Kanta Services (Kanta-palvelut) is Finland's mandatory national digital health/social-care data backbone, operated by Kela (the Social Insurance Institution of Finland) since 2015 (with the ePrescription service running earlier). It already delivers most of the primary-use functions EHDS envisions EU-wide (patient data repository, ePrescription, patient portal, pharmaceuticals data, and — more recently — social welfare data), and Finland is widely cited as one of the EU's most mature national eHealth/interoperability systems, comparable to or ahead of Denmark and Estonia.

### Cited Findings
- Kanta is "a centralised patient data repository for health, wellbeing and social welfare information, maintained by the Social Insurance Institution of Finland (Kela)"; centralized national ePrescription and patient-data-repository services have been in place since 2015 and are described as providing "a solid basis for cross-border healthcare." — [Kanta.fi: EU Electronic Prescriptions and Health Records](https://www.kanta.fi/en/professionals/eu-electronic-prescriptions-and-health-records) (via search snippet)
- Kela is responsible for the Kanta Services, including OmaKanta/MyKanta (the citizen-facing portal for viewing personal health data), and is also responsible for developing Kanta so it complies with EHDS Regulation requirements. — [Kanta – Finland's Digital Health and Social Care Treasure (Kela/Kanta presentation)](https://yhteistyotilat.fi/wiki08/download/attachments/89634798/Kanta%20services%20presentation%20helsenorge%2020220812_final_v4.pdf)
- The Secondary Use of Health and Social Data Act (2019) and the long-developed Kanta Services are described as providing "a solid foundation for the European health data era." — [search synthesis citing Kanta/Kela sources, ~2025](https://www.kanta.fi/en/professionals/priorities-of-the-kanta-services)
- Finland achieves an exceptionally high national interoperability rate (cited as 98.9%) via Kanta, attributed to mandatory participation, strong regulatory enforcement, and widespread HL7 FHIR adoption; European healthcare executives (in a Black Book survey) cite Finland (with Denmark) as a model for EHR interoperability and patient access, with Finland's Kanta platform cited by roughly 90% of surveyed healthcare executives as an inspiration for nationwide interoperability and patient record accessibility. — [Black Book Research survey coverage, Newswire, 2025](https://www.newswire.com/news/european-health-it-leaders-look-to-denmark-and-finland-as-models-for-22588141); [Global Interoperability Gaps Revealed in 2025 Healthcare Connectivity Rankings, Black Book](https://www.newswire.com/news/global-interoperability-gaps-revealed-in-2025-healthcare-connectivity-22560949)
- In earlier European Commission (2013) and WHO (2016) benchmarking surveys, Finland scored ahead of the EU and global average on selected eHealth indicators. — [search synthesis, sourced to European Commission/WHO survey coverage](https://gnius.esante.gouv.fr/en/international-e-health/finland)
- Kanta's roadmap (tiekartta) covers 2025–2029 (with later material referencing a 2026–2030 roadmap), setting out the long-term development of Kanta Services, explicitly including "national implementation of the EHDS regulation" as a long-term development focus alongside new statutory functionalities and structured data for research/decision-making. — [Kanta tiekartta 2026-2030 (Kanta.fi)](https://www.kanta.fi/documents/d/guest/kanta-tiekartta-2026-2030); [THL: Kanta-palvelujen painopisteet on vahvistettu](https://thl.fi/-/kansallista-tietovarantoa-kartutetaan-kanta-palvelujen-painopisteet-on-vahvistettu)
- Kanta's 2026 development priorities include: the Kanta medication list, information flow between social and healthcare services, storage of healthcare appointment data in Kanta, visibility of social-care client records in OmaKanta, and finalizing functional-capacity data and electronic medical certificates; Kanta's data content and interfaces are being gradually migrated to the international HL7 FHIR standard, with the first new FHIR content covering appointment data and cross-sector functional-capacity data. — [Kanta-palvelut vuonna 2026 (Kela press release via STT Info)](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/71758006/kanta-palvelut-vuonna-2026-kannan-tiedot-palvelevat-yha-paremmin-ammattilaisten-tyota) / [Kela.fi](https://www.kela.fi/ajankohtaista/kanta-palvelut-vuonna-2026-kannan-tiedot-palvelevat-yha-paremmin-ammattilaisten-tyota)
- Kanta's development funding is described as "significantly decreasing in 2026" due to public budget cuts, forcing strict prioritization of development work — including EHDS-related work. — [Kanta-palvelut vuonna 2026 (Kela, 2026)](https://www.kela.fi/ajankohtaista/kanta-palvelut-vuonna-2026-kannan-tiedot-palvelevat-yha-paremmin-ammattilaisten-tyota)

### Inferences
- Because Kanta already centralizes patient records, e-prescriptions, and (increasingly) social-care data nationwide with mandatory provider participation, Finland's baseline "primary use" interoperability already satisfies much of what EHDS requires of other member states (patient access, provider-to-provider exchange), which is why Finnish officials frame EHDS largely as an extension/refinement of Kanta rather than a from-scratch build.
- The explicit budget-cut warning for 2026 Kanta development suggests a real risk to the pace of EHDS-related technical work (e.g., FHIR migration, cross-border gateway development) even though the policy commitment is strong.

### Gaps
- No official, single quantitative EU-wide ranking (e.g., an eHealth Network or European Commission scoreboard) placing Finland relative to all 26 other member states was retrieved directly; comparisons above rely on a commercial survey (Black Book) and general commentary rather than an EU official interoperability index.

## What have Kela, THL, and STM announced regarding EHDS implementation, NCPeH status, or a national roadmap?

### Takeaway
The Finnish Ministry of Social Affairs and Health (STM) is formally leading national EHDS implementation, having launched a coordination project (2025–2027) with a steering group and (at least) two working groups, and has issued a public consultation (lausuntopyyntö) on legislative amendments; THL has been assigned by STM to help prepare implementation in 2026 and states that Kanta services are "central" to EHDS implementation; Kela continues to operate the technical infrastructure (Kanta) and the existing cross-border contact point.

### Cited Findings
- STM has initiated the national implementation of the EHDS regulation, "establishing both a steering group and two working groups." — [search synthesis of Kanta.fi/THL/tehdas.eu sources](https://www.iderha.org/news/implementing-ehds-national-level-news-finland)
- STM runs a formal project titled "EHDS-asetuksen kansallisen toimeenpanon koordinaatio 2025–2027" (Coordination of the national implementation of the EHDS Regulation 2025–2027). — [STM project page](https://stm.fi/en/project?tunnus=STM174:00/2025)
- STM issued a public consultation request ("Lausuntopyyntö: Terveystietojen jakamista ja hallintaa helpottava EU-asetus tuo muutoksia lainsäädäntöön" — "Request for comments: EU regulation facilitating the sharing and management of health data brings changes to legislation") on legislative changes needed to implement EHDS. — [STM press release](https://stm.fi/-/lausuntopyynto-terveystietojen-jakamista-ja-hallintaa-helpottava-eu-asetus-tuo-muutoksia-lainsaadantoon)
- A related STM/Government (Valtioneuvosto) press release, "Eurooppalainen terveysdata-avaruusasetus (EHDS) aiheuttaa muutoksia potilastietojen käsittelyyn terveydenhuollossa" ("The European Health Data Space Regulation causes changes to the processing of patient data in healthcare"), states the regulation requires changes to national data-management legislation (Patient Data Act and secondary legislation) so patients' new rights and inter-member-state data exchange can be implemented within the regulation's transition periods; EHDS implementation has been allocated €4.4 million in STM's main budget category. — [Valtioneuvosto/STM press release](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1271139/eurooppalainen-terveysdata-avaruusasetus-ehds-aiheuttaa-muutoksia-potilastietojen-kasittelyyn-terveydenhuollossa)
- A follow-up consultation covers supplementary regulation to (1) the law on electronic processing of social and health care customer data, (2) the law on electronic prescriptions, and (3) the law on market surveillance of certain products; the government proposal was intended for submission to Parliament on the 2025 budget-legislation schedule, with the amended laws intended to enter into force 1 January 2026. — [search synthesis of STM/Edilex/Lausuntopalvelu consultation materials](https://www.lausuntopalvelu.fi/FI/Proposal/Participation?proposalId=3eb57f8f-0b54-4859-9a45-7a47ab676035)
- The same consultation package reportedly addresses patient-record archiving: the National Archives (Kansallisarkisto) could make a selection decision on permanent retention of public healthcare patient data, i.e., determining how extensively patient data would be archived going forward. — [search synthesis of Finnish consultation coverage, 2025](https://www.edilex.fi/asiasanat/Potilasasiakirja)
- THL states that "Kanta services are central in implementing the EHDS Regulation — in cooperation with all actors," and that STM assigned THL the task (for 2026) of preparing EHDS implementation together with other government organizations and cooperation partners; THL's role is to support implementation "through data structures, operational models, and research" and to coordinate cooperation between national actors. — [THL: Kanta services are central in implementing the EHDS Regulation](https://thl.fi/en/-/kanta-services-are-central-in-implementing-the-ehds-regulation-in-cooperation-with-all-actors); [THL blog (Finnish)](https://blogi.thl.fi/kanta-palveluilla-on-keskeinen-rooli-ehds-asetuksen-toimeenpanossa-yhteistyossa-kaikkien-toimijoiden-kanssa/)
- THL frames EHDS development work as covering both primary use (making patient data available for treatment) and secondary use (research, innovation, decision-making), and notes the regulation entered into force 26 March 2025, becomes generally applicable from 26 March 2027, with additional transition periods built in. — [THL EHDS page (Finnish)](https://thl.fi/aiheet/tiedonhallinta-sosiaali-ja-terveysalalla/tiedonhallinnan-ohjaus/sote-tiedonhallinnan-kehittamisnakymia/eurooppalainen-terveystietoalue-ehds-)
- Under the EU Regulation timeline as summarized in Finnish coverage: Member States must designate their Health Data Access Body (HDAB) and one National Contact Point (NCP) by 2027; the European Commission must adopt key implementing acts by March 2027; the full system must be operational by 2029; secondary-use provisions generally apply from March 2029, and from March 2031 for certain data categories. — [search synthesis of EHDS timeline coverage](https://findata.fi/en/services-and-instructions/ehds/)
- STM ran a public webinar, "Eurooppalainen terveysdata-avaruusasetus (EHDS) -webinaari: vaikutukset potilastietojärjestelmien toteutukseen" ("EHDS webinar: impacts on the implementation of patient information systems"), held 7 November 2024 — directly addressing impacts on patient information systems (i.e., HIS/EMR vendors and providers). — [STM event page](https://stm.fi/tapahtumat/2024-11-07/eurooppalainen-terveysdata-avaruusasetus-ehds-webinaari-vaikutukset-potilastietojarjestelmien-toteutukseen)
- A Finnish Medical Journal (Lääkärilehti) article quotes a THL chief physician stating that "every doctor should be interested in EHDS" and that clinicians "have the opportunity to influence what kind of systems they will be using" — indicating THL is engaging clinical/professional stakeholders directly in EHDS preparation. — [Lääkärilehti interview with THL chief physician](https://www.laakarilehti.fi/terveydenhuolto/sinulla-on-mahdollisuus-vaikuttaa-siihen-millaiset-jarjestelmat-sinulla-on-kaytossasi/)

### Inferences
- The division of labor appears to be: STM = policy/legal lead and formal EU-facing coordination (steering group, legislative amendments); THL = technical/operational preparation support (data structures, standards, stakeholder coordination), formally tasked by STM for 2026; Kela = operator of the underlying Kanta infrastructure that will carry most of the primary-use EHDS obligations.
- The 1 January 2026 target for entry into force of amended national laws (Patient Data Act-related, ePrescription law, market surveillance law) suggests Finland is trying to align its own legal baseline ahead of the EU's March 2027 "generally applicable" date, consistent with its frontrunner positioning.

### Gaps
- No official Finnish document could be directly retrieved (fetches to stm.fi, thl.fi, and valtioneuvosto.fi were blocked by network egress restrictions in this environment) confirming the final, as-passed text or enactment date of the amended laws — only that a consultation targeted 1 January 2026 entry into force. The report should flag this as based on a consultation-stage announcement, not confirmed enacted legislation, and should be verified against stm.fi/finlex.fi directly if possible.
- Full membership/output of the STM steering group and the two working groups (e.g., which stakeholders, meeting cadence, deliverables) was not found in available search snippets.
- No official NCPeH designation announcement naming a specific body (Kela vs. THL vs. STM) as Finland's formal EHDS-era "National Contact Point" (distinct from the pre-existing eHDSI/cross-border-care contact point at Kela) was found — see next section for detail on the pre-existing contact point.

## Does Finland already participate in MyHealth@EU / eHDSI cross-border services? What is the current status?

### Takeaway
Yes — Finland is an active, and in some respects pioneering, participant in MyHealth@EU/eHDSI: it was one of the first two countries (with Estonia) to exchange cross-border ePrescriptions (from January 2019), also exchanges Patient Summaries with other MyHealth@EU countries, and Kela operates Finland's National Contact Point for eHealth (NCPeH) function, which predates EHDS. Finland is also expanding into new cross-border services (e.g., laboratory results) ahead of the EHDS mandate.

### Cited Findings
- Finland is one of 25 EU countries currently offering MyHealth@EU electronic cross-border health services. — [European Commission, Electronic cross-border health services](https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/digital-health-and-care/electronic-cross-border-health-services_en)
- The European cross-border ePrescription (CBeP) service was launched in January 2019, initially allowing Finnish patients to fill their Finnish ePrescriptions at community pharmacies in Estonia; by June 2020, Estonian ePrescriptions became dispensable in Finnish pharmacies as well, making Estonia and Finland pioneering countries for bidirectional cross-border ePrescription use. — [search synthesis of EC/MyHealth@EU documentation](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/d4e2343d-fa08-4df9-baeb-b01536ba042f_en)
- Patient Summaries (covering allergies, current medication, past illnesses, and surgeries) are exchanged to give treating clinicians essential information, in their own language, about patients visiting from another MyHealth@EU country — Finland participates in this exchange. — [EC MyHealth@EU documentation](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/d4e2343d-fa08-4df9-baeb-b01536ba042f_en)
- MyHealth@EU relies on TESTA, the secure European data-communication network (supported under the Digital Europe Programme) used for exchanging sensitive information between public authorities in EU countries. — [search synthesis, EC/Interreg Europe documentation](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/egovernment/solution/testa/news/secure-and-efficient-use-health-data)
- Finland is developing FLaReS, a Laboratory Result Report Service, to enable cross-border exchange of laboratory data — benefiting both traveling Finnish citizens and visitors seeking care in Finland — as an extension beyond the current ePrescription/Patient Summary scope. — [search synthesis of EU4Health/MyHealth@EU project coverage](https://www.openaccessgovernment.org/new-eu4health-projects-push-digital-health-forward-across-europe/202153/)
- Kela's Contact Point for Cross-Border Health Care has operated within Kela since 2014, providing information on using health services in Finland and abroad (reachable at yhteyspiste@kela.fi, responding within 10 working days). — [Kela: Contact Point for Cross-Border Health Care](https://www.kela.fi/contact-point)
- "Kela produces the eHDSI National Contact Point for eHealth" as part of Finland's broader digital health infrastructure, with Kanta described as "the national backbone of Finnish eHealth services." — [search synthesis citing Kela/Digital Health Finland sources](https://gnius.esante.gouv.fr/en/international-e-health/finland)
- Kanta.fi published a Finnish-language explainer, "Potilastietojen kirjaaminen Euroopassa yhdenmukaistuu – mikä muuttuu?" ("Recording of patient data in Europe is becoming harmonized — what will change?"), and a related item stating "the prescription and patient data of Finns can now be utilised in new European countries," describing further roll-out of cross-border prescription/patient data usability. — [Kanta.fi (Finnish)](https://www.kanta.fi/ajankohtaista/potilastietojen-kirjaaminen-euroopassa-yhdenmukaistuu-mika-muuttuu-); [EU-terveydenhoito.fi coverage](https://www.eu-healthcare.fi/whats-new/the-prescription-and-patient-data-of-finns-can-now-be-utilised-in-new-european-countries/)
- Kanta services are described as working on functioning data exchange across health and social care, "with HL7 FHIR profiling underway and integration into national data repositories planned to start in 2026" (in the cross-border/EHDS context). — [search synthesis of Kanta.fi/IDERHA coverage](https://www.iderha.org/news/implementing-ehds-national-level-news-finland)

### Inferences
- Because Kela already runs the pre-EHDS eHDSI NCPeH function, Finland is well positioned to either extend this existing organizational role into the EHDS-era "National Contact Point for Digital Health" or to have that formal role sit alongside/within Kela's existing Kanta operations — although no source explicitly confirms this continuity is legally settled (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No source explicitly confirms whether the EHDS Regulation's formal "National Contact Point for Digital Health" (NCPeH under EHDS Chapter III) has been formally, legally designated as Kela (vs. a new/different body) — Kela's role described above is under the pre-existing eHDSI framework, and while continuity seems highly likely, an explicit post-March-2025 legal designation announcement was not found in available search results.
- Exact current number of EU countries exchanging Patient Summaries/ePrescriptions with Finland (bilateral country list, beyond Estonia) was not confirmed in detail from primary sources due to fetch access restrictions.

## Who are the major HIS/EMR vendors and systems in Finnish hospitals/primary care, and have any vendors or hospital districts discussed EHDS integration plans?

### Takeaway
Finland's HIS/EMR market is now consolidated mainly around three vendors — TietoEvry (Lifecare), CGI (Pegasos/Uranus legacy, now consolidating into OMNI360), and Mediconsult — serving the country's wellbeing services counties (hyvinvointialueet), while the Helsinki-Uusimaa region runs a separate Epic-based system (Apotti) built for HUS and partner municipalities. Public confirmation of vendor-specific EHDS technical integration plans is thin in available sources; the clearest EHDS-vendor link found is that STM held a dedicated webinar on EHDS impacts for patient information system implementation, and Kanta.fi maintains system-developer-facing status pages that will be the practical channel for vendor changes.

### Cited Findings
- Effica, Pegasos, Uranus, Lifecare, and Mediatri are named among the most commonly used patient/customer information systems in Finland; older systems Effica, Pegasos, and Uranus have been undergoing replacement/consolidation ("Effica, Pegasos ja Uranus jäävät historiaan" — "Effica, Pegasos and Uranus will become history"). — [Mediuutiset/Kauppalehti](https://www.mediuutiset.fi/uutiset/effica-pegasos-ja-uranus-jaavat-historiaan/60666593-f4d8-3476-9d8f-d59970718586)
- TietoEvry's Lifecare system replaced Effica as a leading customer/patient information system; CGI's Pegasos and Uranus systems were being consolidated with a shared "Desktop" user interface, and CGI's current-generation system is branded OMNI360 (marketed as "OMNI360 – a Finnish patient information system for people," CGI Finland). — [search synthesis of Finnish HIS vendor coverage](https://www.cgi.com/fi/fi/tuoteratkaisut/omni360-asiakas-ja-potilastietojarjestelma)
- Recent regional (wellbeing services county / hyvinvointialue) HIS procurements: Pohjanmaa wellbeing region selected TietoEvry's Lifecare as its new customer/patient information system; Pirkanmaa and Pohjois-Savo regions selected CGI's OMNI360; East Uusimaa and West Uusimaa (Itä-Uudenmaan and Länsi-Uudenmaan hyvinvointialue) processes involved bids from CGI, Mediconsult, and TietoEvry, with TietoEvry winning at least the East Uusimaa tender; Central Finland (Keski-Suomi) is moving toward one shared patient information system. — [Pohjanmaan hyvinvointialue](https://pohjanmaanhyvinvointi.fi/tietoevry-toimittaa-uuden-asiakas-ja-potilastietojarjestelman/); [Pirha.fi (Pirkanmaa)](https://www.pirha.fi/w/cgi-n-jarjestelma-valittu-uudeksi-potilastietojarjestelmaksi-pirkanmaalle); [Itä-Uudenmaan hyvinvointialue](https://itauusimaa.fi/2024/12/18/ita-uudenmaan-hyvinvointialue-valitsi-tietoevryn-asiakas-ja-potilastietojarjestelman-toimittajakseen/); [Länsi-Uudenmaan hyvinvointialue (STT Info)](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70771534/lansi-uudenmaan-hyvinvointialue-valitsi-lifecare-kokonaisuuden-asiakas-ja-potilastietojarjestelmakseen); [Keski-Suomen hyvinvointialue](https://www.hyvaks.fi/uutiset/keski-suomen-hyvinvointialueelle-tulossa-yksi-yhteinen-potilastietojarjestelma)
- Three vendors — CGI, Mediconsult, and TietoEvry — are described as the systems "widely used in social and healthcare services in Finland," confirming these three as the dominant current HIS/EMR vendor set for Finland's 21 wellbeing services counties. — [search synthesis of Finnish HIS procurement coverage, 2024–2025]
- Apotti is a joint EHR project of the City of Helsinki, Vantaa, Kirkkonummi, and Kauniainen together with the Hospital District of Helsinki and Uusimaa (HUS); HUS implemented an Epic-based EHR (branded Apotti) in four phases during 2018–2021; the system consists of a core Epic entity plus supplementary systems/integrations, and Apotti is described as integrated into "one unified entity" enabling joint data use and open interfaces in real time "within the boundaries of legislation." — [Apotti — the Apotti ecosystem (search synthesis)](https://www.apotti.fi/en/the-apotti-ecosystem/); [PMC: Implementing a New EHR System in a University Hospital](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9222436/)
- FHIR is recommended as the interface technology for Apotti integrations, but at least one documented interoperability limitation exists: HUS specialized medical care cannot search a patient's primary-care information within the Apotti system due to current system limitations — an internal (not cross-border) interoperability gap. — [search synthesis of Apotti technical literature](https://grokipedia.com/page/Apotti)
- STM held a public webinar specifically titled "Eurooppalainen terveysdata-avaruusasetus (EHDS) -webinaari: vaikutukset potilastietojärjestelmien toteutukseen" (7 November 2024), i.e., a session explicitly about EHDS impacts on patient information system implementation — directly aimed at the HIS/EMR ecosystem (vendors, IT leads, provider organizations). — [STM event listing](https://stm.fi/tapahtumat/2024-11-07/eurooppalainen-terveysdata-avaruusasetus-ehds-webinaari-vaikutukset-potilastietojarjestelmien-toteutukseen)
- Kanta.fi maintains a "Patient Data Repository status" page for system developers ("Potilastietovarannon tilanne"), the practical technical/status channel through which HIS vendors track connection requirements to Kanta — a likely future channel for EHDS-driven interface changes as well. — [Kanta.fi (Finnish, system developers section)](https://www.kanta.fi/jarjestelmakehittajat/pta-tilanne)
- A 2020 national situational analysis of customer and patient information systems ("Asiakas- ja potilastietojärjestelmien tilannekuva ja sen analyysi 2020") produced via the Association of Finnish Local and Regional Authorities (Kuntaliitto)/AKUSTI provides a structured map of which systems (Effica, Pegasos, Uranus, Lifecare, Mediatri, Apotti, etc.) were in use where as of 2020, useful as a pre-reform baseline. — [Kuntaliitto/AKUSTI report (PDF)](https://www.kuntaliitto.fi/sites/default/files/media/file/APTJ-tilannekuva2020_AKUSTI110620_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- With HIS provision now concentrated in essentially three vendors (TietoEvry, CGI, Mediconsult) plus the separate Epic/Apotti installation in Helsinki-Uusimaa, EHDS-driven European Electronic Health Record exchange format (EEHRxF) and FHIR-based interface requirements will functionally need to be implemented by a small number of vendor engineering teams plus Kela's Kanta interfaces — a narrower vendor-coordination problem than in more fragmented EU markets, which may work in Finland's favor for meeting EHDS interoperability deadlines, but the recent wave of regional system replacements (2023-2025) suggests significant vendor engineering bandwidth is currently absorbed by migration projects rather than being free for EHDS-specific work.
- No vendor (TietoEvry, CGI, Mediconsult, Apotti/Epic) was found in search results to have made a public EHDS-specific statement, roadmap, or technical announcement — this appears to be a genuine information gap rather than an oversight in search strategy (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No direct public statement from TietoEvry, CGI, Mediconsult, or the Apotti alliance/HUS specifically describing their EHDS compliance roadmap, EEHRxF adoption plan, or gateway/interoperability project was found in available search results. This is a notable gap — outreach directly to these vendors' press/investor-relations channels, or to Kanta.fi's system-developer bulletins, would be needed to confirm vendor-level EHDS plans.
- Could not confirm from primary sources (kanta.fi, apotti.fi, cgi.com, tietoevry.com fetches were blocked by network egress restrictions in this research environment) whether Apotti/Epic has any EHDS- or EEHRxF-specific integration project underway; only general Kanta/FHIR integration was found.

## Is THL or Kela Finland's national contact point for digital health / national access point for secondary use data? Has a Finnish Health Data Access Body (HDAB) been designated — is Findata the candidate?

### Takeaway
Findata (the Finnish Social and Health Data Permit Authority) is consistently described by itself and by EU-facing coordination projects as the "closest equivalent" to — and de facto candidate for — Finland's EHDS Health Data Access Body (HDAB), but as of the available sources, no source confirms a final, formal legal designation decision has been made; Kela continues to operate the primary-use-side National Contact Point (eHDSI/NCPeH) function, distinct from the secondary-use HDAB role.

### Cited Findings
- "In Finland, the closest equivalent to the EHDS Health Data Access Body is currently Findata. More specifically, Findata serves as Finland's data permit authority and fulfills the role of the Health Data Access Body (HDAB) under the EHDS Regulation, operating as a 'one-stop shop' for data access." — [search synthesis of Findata.fi EHDS page](https://findata.fi/en/services-and-instructions/ehds/)
- "No decisions have yet been made regarding national implementation, and national regulation does not yet include all the requirements set by EHDS" — directly qualifying the above statement and indicating Findata's HDAB role is anticipated/de facto rather than finally, formally confirmed in national law as of the source's writing. — [search synthesis of Findata.fi EHDS page](https://findata.fi/en/services-and-instructions/ehds/)
- Findata was established in 2019 following the entry into force of the Act on the Secondary Use of Health and Social Data (Secondary Use Act), making Finland "one of the first EU countries with a dedicated health data permit authority." — [search synthesis of Findata.fi "About Findata" materials](https://findata.fi/en/)
- Findata functions include granting data permits, collecting and pseudonymizing data, and setting requirements for secure environments for data processing — the same core functions the EHDS Regulation assigns to national HDABs. — [search synthesis of Findata.fi materials](https://findata.fi/en/about-findata/your-data-rights/)
- Findata is participating in the EU's "Community of Practice," a collaborative network of national authorities (established January 2024) supporting secondary use of health data as part of EHDS implementation — indicating Findata is already operating in the EU-level HDAB coordination structure even ahead of formal national designation. — [search synthesis of Findata.fi/EU coordination materials](https://findata.fi/en/faq/what-are-the-secondary-use-purposes-under-the-ehds-regulation/)
- Findata is developing Finland's secondary-use technical infrastructure through the FinHITS project (FinHITS = "Strengthening Finnish Health Data ICT for Secondary Use"), a four-year EU-co-funded project (2023–2027) explicitly intended to "prepare Finland for the requirements of the European Health Data Space (EHDS), such as cross-border infrastructure." Work under FinHITS includes Findata's e-service, the national data catalogue, data-quality tooling, and — specifically for EHDS — connecting Findata's e-service and national dataset catalogue to the HealthData@EU cross-border infrastructure; a new e-service to streamline data-permit applications is planned for launch in autumn 2026. — [Findata: FinHITS project page](https://findata.fi/en/finhits/); [search synthesis of FinHITS coverage](https://www.spinverse.com/references/finhits-strengthen-finnish-health-data-project-32)
- As of the FinHITS-related sources, Finland already has ten EHDS-compliant secure data processing environments, with approximately 5,000 users and about 1,100 ongoing research projects using Findata-permitted secondary-use data. — [search synthesis of Findata/FinHITS reporting](https://findata.fi/en/news/findata-developes-finnish-secondary-use-services-in-the-finhits-project/)
- Kela's pre-existing "Contact Point for Cross-Border Health Care" (operating since 2014) and the statement that "Kela produces the eHDSI National Contact Point for eHealth" indicate Kela — not THL, not Findata — holds the primary-use-side, cross-border national contact point function. — [Kela: Contact Point for Cross-Border Health Care](https://www.kela.fi/contact-point); [search synthesis](https://gnius.esante.gouv.fr/en/international-e-health/finland)

### Inferences
- The functional split appears to be: Kela = National Contact Point for Digital Health / primary-use cross-border exchange (continuing its pre-EHDS eHDSI NCPeH role); Findata = Health Data Access Body / secondary-use national access point (continuing and expanding its Secondary Use Act role). This mirrors the EHDS Regulation's own structural distinction between primary-use NCPeH and secondary-use HDAB functions, and is consistent with each body's existing legal mandate — but should be flagged in the final report as the most likely, not yet a confirmed-final, arrangement.
- Findata's "one-stop shop" positioning and active participation in EU Community of Practice / FinHITS's HealthData@EU integration work strongly suggest Finland is treating Findata's HDAB designation as effectively settled in practice, even if a final statutory instrument formally naming it under Finland's revised Secondary Use Act had not been confirmed as enacted in the sources reviewed.

### Gaps
- No source explicitly confirms a final legal/statutory act formally designating Findata as Finland's EHDS Health Data Access Body (as opposed to it being the strong de facto/expected candidate). This is an important open item to flag for the report as "highly likely but not confirmed as formally finalized" based on available sources (findata.fi's own EHDS page, dated at time of retrieval, explicitly says "no decisions have yet been made regarding national implementation").
- No official Finnish-government document text (STM, Finlex) was directly retrievable in this session (egress restrictions) confirming NCPeH designation-in-law for Kela under the EHDS Regulation specifically (versus its pre-existing eHDSI operational role).

## What existing Finnish legislation already anticipates/aligns with EHDS secondary-use provisions?

### Takeaway
Finland's 2019 Act on the Secondary Use of Health and Social Data (the "Secondary Use Act," laki sosiaali- ja terveystietojen toissijaisesta käytöstä) — which created Findata — is repeatedly cited as already providing much of the legal and institutional foundation the EHDS Regulation requires for secondary use, and this Act is now undergoing a reform process to bring it fully into line with EHDS requirements.

### Cited Findings
- Findata's legal basis is the Act on the Secondary Use of Health and Social Data (2019), commonly called the "Secondary Use Act," which established Findata as the national permit authority for secondary use of social and healthcare data. — [Findata: Legislation](https://findata.fi/en/services-and-instructions/legislation/)
- Sources explicitly describe "the Secondary Use of Health and Social Data Act (2019) and the long-developed Kanta Services" as together providing "a solid foundation for the European health data era" — i.e., positioning existing Finnish secondary-use law as EHDS-ready groundwork rather than something built from scratch. — [search synthesis of Kanta/Kela EHDS-era materials](https://www.kanta.fi/en/professionals/priorities-of-the-kanta-services)
- A reform of the Finnish Act on the Secondary Use of Social and Health Data is underway/discussed by Finnish legal commentators, indicating the existing 2019 Act is being actively amended to align with (and does not yet fully match) EHDS's secondary-use requirements. — [Dittmar & Indrenius Attorneys: The reform of the Finnish Act on the Secondary Use of Social and Health Data](https://www.dittmar.fi/news/the-reform-of-the-finnish-act-on-the-secondary-use-of-social-and-health-data/) (title/topic confirmed via search; full text not retrievable due to egress block)
- The EHDS Regulation's secondary-use provisions apply generally from March 2029 (March 2031 for certain data categories), giving Finland a multi-year window to complete this legislative alignment. — [search synthesis of EHDS timeline coverage](https://findata.fi/en/services-and-instructions/ehds/)
- Separately, on the primary-use/patient-data side, STM's 2025 consultation package proposed amendments to (1) the law on electronic processing of social and health care customer data, (2) the law on electronic prescriptions, and (3) the law on market surveillance of certain products, targeting entry into force of 1 January 2026 — showing Finland is also amending its Patient Data Act-adjacent framework (not just the Secondary Use Act) to align with EHDS primary-use obligations. — [search synthesis of STM/Lausuntopalvelu consultation materials](https://www.lausuntopalvelu.fi/FI/Proposal/Participation?proposalId=3eb57f8f-0b54-4859-9a45-7a47ab676035)

### Inferences
- Finland's approach appears to be incremental amendment of two existing, mature legal frameworks (the Secondary Use Act for HDAB/secondary-use alignment; the electronic patient/customer data and ePrescription laws for primary-use alignment) rather than wholesale new legislation — consistent with Finland's frontrunner positioning and its "Kanta + Secondary Use Act as foundation" narrative.

### Gaps
- The specific substantive content of the Secondary Use Act reform (which provisions are being changed, and whether it explicitly names Findata as the EHDS HDAB) could not be confirmed from the Dittmar & Indrenius article or any Finlex/STM source due to egress restrictions blocking direct fetch of stm.fi, findata.fi, dittmar.fi, and valtioneuvosto.fi in this research environment. This should be verified directly against Finlex.fi or a direct (non-blocked) fetch of the Dittmar article and Findata's legislation page for the final report.
