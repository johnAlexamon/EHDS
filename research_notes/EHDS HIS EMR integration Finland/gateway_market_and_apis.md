## KQ1: Technical architecture of MyHealth@EU/eHDSI (primary use) and HealthData@EU (secondary use); is there an NCPeH gateway in every member state; who operates it; is it open to competition?

### Takeaway
MyHealth@EU (eHDSI) is a **federated architecture**: the European Commission runs shared central/crosscutting ICT services (terminology, configuration, cross-border trust), and each Member State builds and operates exactly **one National Contact Point for eHealth (NCPeH)** that connects to it — this is a single national gateway per country, not a competitive multi-vendor field. HealthData@EU (secondary use) is a separate, newer node-based network connecting national Health Data Access Bodies (HDABs)/health data hubs to an EU Central Platform via the eDelivery AS4 building block.

### Cited Findings
- MyHealth@EU's architecture is federated: each Member State builds and operates its own National Contact Point for eHealth (NCPeH), functioning as a national gateway; the NCPeH exposes the country's own eHealth services abroad, exposes other countries' services domestically, and manages authentication, consent, and semantic/syntactic matching between national and EU data variants — [search synthesis of noze.it / NCPeH wiki / eHealth Network guidance](https://www.noze.it/en/insights/ips-myhealth-eu-crossborder/), [NCPeH CY](https://ncpeh.cy/en/national-contact-points/)
- Communication between two NCPeHs uses a SOAP/REST request carrying a SAML assertion attesting the requester's identity, country, and legal basis — [search synthesis, primary source not directly verified]
- The European Commission provides unified, central ICT infrastructure and cross-cutting services (terminology, configuration, interoperability), and EU countries build National Contact Points for eHealth on top of these central services to join the broader network — [search synthesis](https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/digital-health-and-care/electronic-cross-border-health-services_en)
- Member States are required to establish and operate the NCPeH following eHealth Network guidelines; NCPeH deployment/operation is coordinated via the eHealth DSI EU countries Expert Group (eHMSEG), with one nominated manager per participating country — [eHealth Network guideline document](https://www.ncpehealth.gr/files/04_guideline_on_an_organizational_framework_for_ncpeh.pdf)
- Concrete national examples of NCPeH operators: **France** — the NCPeH "Sesali" was created and is operated by ANS (Agence du Numérique en Santé) under mandate of the French Ministry of Health — [ANS/esante.gouv.fr](https://ue.esante.gouv.fr/defining-european-ehealth-framework-and-contributing-common-approach/ncpeh-sesali); **Cyprus** — the NCPeH platform is operated/supported by the National Electronic Health Authority (NeHA), a national public authority — [NeHA Cyprus](https://neha.org.cy/en/national-contact-points/)
- HealthData@EU (secondary use) is a network of nodes representing different national data brokers, holders and consumers, supported by central services provided jointly by Member States and the European Commission; identified pilot nodes include BBMRI, Health Data Lab (Germany), Danish Health Data Authority (Denmark), Findata (Finland), Health Data Hub (France), Sciensano (Belgium), Norwegian Directorate of eHealth (Norway), and the Croatian Institute of Public Health (Croatia) — [PMC — Piloting an infrastructure for secondary use of health data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12420900/)
- HealthData@EU's technical components are: (1) information systems for national metadata catalogues, (2) information systems for cross-border data access applications/requests, and (3) secure processing environments; the infrastructure uses the EU's eDelivery Building Block (AS4 protocol) for secure machine-to-machine connectivity between the Central Platform and National Contact Points — [PMC / EU Digital Building Blocks](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=592643692)
- Release 3 of the eDelivery AS4 cross-border gateway introduced the "HealthData@EU Health Data Dispatcher," which replaced the earlier EHDS2 Pilot connector while keeping the same API — [search synthesis of EU Digital Building Blocks pages](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/x/T2bkN)

### Inferences (ANALYSIS, not confirmed fact)
- The NCPeH model is structurally a **national monopoly gateway per country** (one designated national contact point), not an open multi-vendor competitive layer at the "which gateway do I connect to" level. However, **vendor competition happens one level down**: the national eHealth agency/ministry that owns the NCPeH mandate typically procures the underlying software/integration platform through public tenders, so IT vendors compete to be *the* contracted technology supplier/system integrator behind a given country's NCPeH, rather than operating parallel competing gateways. This matches the pattern seen in France (ANS as public operator, likely contracting technology suppliers) and Cyprus (NeHA as public operator).
- This national-monopoly-gateway-with-vendor-competition-for-the-contract pattern is analogous to, and likely to be reused for, the new national EHDS primary-use and secondary-use touchpoints — i.e., a single national gateway/HDAB per country, with the technology stack behind it awarded via national procurement to established health-IT integration vendors.

## KQ2: Will every hospital/HIS need a direct connection to national infrastructure, or will there be a small number of centralized national gateways (like Kanta in Finland)? Is this architecture specified by the Regulation, or left to Member States?

### Takeaway
Existing precedent (Finland's Kanta) and emerging design descriptions of national EHDS layers both point toward a **small number of centralized national hub(s)/gateways** that hospitals and other HIS/EHR systems connect into, rather than every hospital independently building a connection to EU-level infrastructure — but the EHDS Regulation itself, as reviewed, appears to set the *obligations and interoperability requirements* rather than mandate one specific national internal architecture, leaving the concrete national wiring largely to Member States.

### Cited Findings
- In Finland, Kanta is a centralized national patient data repository maintained by Kela (Finland's Social Insurance Institution); social and healthcare providers are legally mandated to connect to Kanta, and all public healthcare providers have been connected to the Patient Data Repository since 2014 (private providers must connect if they keep long-term electronic patient records); data entered in a local EHR is automatically transferred to the central repository — [Findata.fi](https://findata.fi/en/course/finnish-health-care-system/lessons/kanta-services-patient-data-repository/)
- Kanta itself serves as Finland's National Contact Point (NCP) for the safe electronic cross-border transfer of health data with other EU countries, and THL states Kanta services are "central" to implementing the EHDS Regulation in Finland — [THL](https://thl.fi/en/-/kanta-services-are-central-in-implementing-the-ehds-regulation-in-cooperation-with-all-actors)
- A Tehdas (Finland) country-visit factsheet found Finland has "almost all elements in place for joining the EHDS," but flagged that resources will be needed to expand national infrastructure capacity to meet increased EHDS-driven data-sharing demand — [Tehdas Finland factsheet, PDF](https://tehdas.eu/app/uploads/2023/04/finland-country-visit-factsheet-04-2023.pdf)
- A described (search-synthesized, not directly fetched) architectural pattern for national EHDS implementation layers describes: a national layer sitting between hospital/clinical systems and the pan-EU MyHealth@EU infrastructure, comprising (a) a shared care record providing a clinically coherent normalized data foundation (often built on openEHR), (b) a vendor/EHR integration layer that federates and harmonizes outputs from diverse hospital EHR systems, and (c) a national EHDS gateway that exposes a unified FHIR façade applying semantic and consent rules before data crosses the border — [search synthesis of better.care blog content, primary source blocked from direct fetch](https://www.better.care/blog-en/ehds-infrastructure-platforms/)
- For the secondary-use side, national IT architecture is described (via search synthesis) as having three conceptual national components: "Cross-Border Engines" integrated into the national Health Data Access Body (HDAB), and a "National Connector" and "Cross-Border Gateway" integrated into the National Contact Point — [search synthesis, likely sourced from a Tehdas/EU architecture deliverable](https://tehdas.eu/results/new-specification-supports-member-state-connection-to-healthdataeu/)
- The EHDS Regulation (EU) 2025/327 requires the Commission to adopt, by 26 March 2027, "common specifications" via implementing acts covering the essential requirements in Annex II (interoperability/security requirements for EHR systems), including a common template and implementation timeline; where these common specifications touch medical devices, in-vitro diagnostics, or high-risk AI systems, consultation with the Medical Device Coordination Group, the European AI Board, and the European Data Protection Board may precede adoption — [search synthesis of EHDS Regulation Article 36 ("Common specifications"), primary text not directly fetched; authoritative text at EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng)

### Inferences (ANALYSIS, not confirmed fact)
- Given (a) the precedent of Kanta as a single national hub model already explicitly identified as central to Finland's EHDS compliance, and (b) descriptions of a three-layer national architecture (shared care record / vendor integration layer / national EHDS gateway) appearing in vendor commentary, the most probable realistic architecture in most Member States is: **a small number of centralized national gateway(s)/hub(s) per country** (in many cases the pre-existing national eHealth infrastructure, extended) that hospital HIS/EMR systems connect to, rather than each hospital independently building and certifying its own cross-border/EU-level connection. This is a reasonable inference from precedent and vendor descriptions, not a confirmed regulatory mandate.
- Based on the "common specifications by 2027" provision, the EHDS Regulation itself appears to set *what* interoperability requirements EHR systems and national infrastructure must meet, but the *internal* national wiring (how many gateways, which agency runs them, whether hospitals connect to one national hub or several) looks to be left to Member States to implement, consistent with the federated NCPeH precedent under MyHealth@EU. This is an inference, not a verified textual reading of the full Regulation.

### Gaps
- I could not directly fetch or verify the exact operative text of the EHDS Regulation's articles on national architecture/hospital connectivity (EUR-Lex full text and the streamlex.eu Article 36 breakdown were not directly retrievable in this session; findings above rely on search-engine synthesis of those sources). A follow-up should directly read Articles 5–15 (EHR systems, Annex II essential requirements) and Article 36 (common specifications) of Regulation (EU) 2025/327 at eur-lex.europa.eu to confirm whether the Regulation textually mandates or merely permits a centralized national gateway model.

## KQ5: Current status of EHDS technical specifications — is EEHRxF finalized? What role do CEN/CENELEC, HL7 Europe, IHE Europe, and Commission implementing acts play?

### Takeaway
The European Electronic Health Record Exchange Format (EEHRxF) is **not yet finalized** — it is expected to become formally established only by around March 2027 via Commission implementing acts, and is currently under active collaborative development by multiple EU-funded initiatives (X-eHealth, XpanDH, HL7 Europe/IHE Europe's joint EURIDICE initiative) rather than being a fixed, stable specification today (late 2025/2026).

### Cited Findings
- The EHDS Regulation (in force since 26 March 2025) requires EHR systems across the EU to adopt harmonized interoperability and logging components using the EEHRxF — [search synthesis of Frontiers in Medicine policy brief](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2025.1648170/full)
- "The EEHRxF will become formally established by March 2027. Until then, the European EEHRxF is still under collaborative development, and certainty about its specifications cannot be provided before the formal publication of the Implementing Acts by the European Commission." — Frontiers in Medicine policy brief, "Accelerating European electronic health record exchange format (EEHRxF) implementation across Europe: a policy perspective," published 9 September 2025 — [Frontiers/PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40995096/)
- A joint action on primary use of health data, funded under the EU4Health work programme 2022, aims to provide recommendations for a formal description of the EEHRxF — [same Frontiers policy brief]
- Multiple EU-funded initiatives continue to contribute to EEHRxF development, including the MyHealth@EU infrastructure, and the X-eHealth and XpanDH projects, alongside projects funded under the Horizon Europe call HORIZON-HLTH-2023-IND-06-02 — [same Frontiers policy brief]
- HL7 Europe has published (announced 2 January 2026) new HL7 Europe FHIR Implementation Guides specifically designed to support EHDS: Base and Core FHIR IGs at "Standard for Trial Use" (STU) status for both FHIR Release 4 and Release 5, plus an HL7 Europe Extensions package — [HL7 News, Jan 2, 2026](https://hl7news.hl7.org/2026/01/02/new-hl7-europe-fhir-implementation-guides-to-support-the-european-health-data-space/)
- These HL7 Europe Base/Core FHIR IGs provide a layered approach: loosely-constrained "Base" profiles for common European concepts (e.g. Patient, Practitioner), with "Core" and further derived/national profiles built on top — [HL7 Europe Base and Core FHIR IG](https://build.fhir.org/ig/hl7-eu/base/introduction.html)
- IHE Europe and HL7 Europe are jointly running an initiative called "EURIDICE" (European Interoperability Specifications for Digital Solutions in Healthcare), where every accepted proposal becomes a joint specification combining the IHE profiling methodology with FHIR technical expertise, explicitly aimed at producing community-validated technical artifacts to support EHDS requirements — [EURIDICE](https://euridice.org/), [IHE Europe white paper, Jan 2025](https://www.ihe-europe.net/sites/default/files/2025-01/IHE-Europe%20-%20White%20Paper%20-%20Leveraging%20IHE%20Methodology%20to%20accelerate_v8.pdf)
- CEN/TC 251 is the CEN technical committee responsible for standardization in health informatics/ICT in the EU, with the explicit remit of achieving compatibility and interoperability between independent systems and enabling modularity in EHR systems — [CEN/TC 251 Business Plan](https://standards.cencenelec.eu/BPCEN/6232.pdf)
- Standardization "requests" (formerly called "mandates") are formal, legally binding contracts between the European Commission and CEN/CENELEC (and sometimes ETSI) in support of legislative work, with contractually binding deliverable deadlines — [CEN-CENELEC guidance on standardization requests/mandates](https://boss.cenelec.eu/reference-material/guidancedoc/pages/mandates/)
- Under the Regulation, by 26 March 2027 the European Commission must adopt "common specifications" via implementing acts covering the essential requirements set out in Annex II of the Regulation (covering EHR system interoperability and security requirements), including a common template and an implementation time limit; where these specifications intersect medical devices, in-vitro diagnostics, or high-risk AI systems, the Commission may consult the Medical Device Coordination Group, the European Artificial Intelligence Board, and the European Data Protection Board first — [search synthesis of EHDS Regulation, Article 36 "Common specifications"; authoritative text at EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng)

### Inferences (ANALYSIS, not confirmed fact)
- The overall picture is of a **multi-track, not-yet-converged standards process**: HL7 Europe and IHE Europe are producing draft/trial-use technical implementation guides (FHIR IGs, EURIDICE joint specifications) now (through 2025–2026), which are likely to feed into — but are not yet identical to — the Commission's eventual legally-binding "common specifications" due by March 2027. CEN/CENELEC's formal standardization-mandate role in this specific EHDS process was not directly confirmed by name in the sources found (no EHDS-specific CEN/CENELEC mandate document was located), so its concrete contribution to EHDS technical specs (as opposed to general EU health informatics standardization work via CEN/TC 251) remains unconfirmed and should be treated as a gap.
- Because Standard-for-Trial-Use (STU) status explicitly signals "not yet final and subject to change," a vendor building against the current HL7 Europe FHIR IGs today (2026) is building against material that is likely to see structural changes before the Commission's common specifications are legally finalized (target: March 2027).

### Gaps
- No source found that names a specific CEN/CENELEC standardization mandate number tied explicitly to the EHDS Regulation (as opposed to CEN/TC 251's general, longstanding remit in health informatics). A follow-up should check the CEN/CENELEC "Standardization requests" register directly for a request tied to Regulation (EU) 2025/327.

## KQ6: Realistic timeline before specs are stable enough for a vendor to build a production connector without high risk of rework

### Takeaway
Based on the confirmed EEHRxF and common-specifications timeline, vendors building EHDS connectors before roughly **March 2027** (when the Commission's legally binding common specifications are due) are working against draft/trial-use material (HL7 Europe STU FHIR IGs, EURIDICE joint specifications) that carries meaningful rework risk; a materially lower-risk window likely does not open until after the common specifications are formally adopted, and possibly not until national implementing/certification schemes built on top of them stabilize shortly after.

### Cited Findings
- "The EEHRxF will become formally established by March 2027. Until then, the European EEHRxF is still under collaborative development, and certainty about its specifications cannot be provided before the formal publication of the Implementing Acts by the European Commission." — [Frontiers in Medicine policy brief, Sept 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40995096/)
- The Commission is required (per search-synthesized reading of Article 36 of the Regulation) to adopt common specifications for EHR system essential requirements (Annex II) by 26 March 2027, via implementing acts that will also set a common template and an implementation time limit — meaning a further compliance grace period likely follows the March 2027 adoption date, rather than immediate applicability — [search synthesis, EUR-Lex authoritative source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng)
- HL7 Europe's newest FHIR Implementation Guides supporting EHDS (published/announced January 2026) are explicitly at "Standard for Trial Use" (STU) status, a formal designation meaning the specification is a draft for real-world testing and feedback and is expected to change before being finalized as a normative standard — [HL7 News, Jan 2026](https://hl7news.hl7.org/2026/01/02/new-hl7-europe-fhir-implementation-guides-to-support-the-european-health-data-space/)
- One secondary source (via search synthesis of an EY/Sidley-style regulatory summary) stated that "by January 2026, all healthcare providers and EHR vendors must certify their systems for interoperability and security compliance" — however, this claim appears to conflict with the confirmed finding above that the Commission's own common specifications (the legal basis for such certification) are not due until March 2027, and with EEHRxF not being "formally established" until March 2027. **This is a direct contradiction between sources that could not be resolved in this research pass** — the "January 2026 certification" claim should be treated with caution and flagged to the report writer as unverified/possibly inaccurate, pending direct confirmation from the EHDS Regulation's transitional/applicability provisions (Article on entry into application, likely to be several years after entry into force for EHR-system-related obligations).
