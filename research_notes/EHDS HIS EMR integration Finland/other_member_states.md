# EHDS Implementation Beyond Finland: Country-by-Country Comparison (Germany, France, Estonia, Netherlands, Austria, Denmark, Sweden, Italy, Spain)

Scope note: This note covers Regulation (EU) 2025/327 (EHDS), which entered into force 26 March 2025. Per multiple consistent sources, most primary-use obligations become applicable from 26 March 2027, most of the secondary-use framework from 26 March 2029, with full application (all Member States, all systems) required by 2031 — [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Germany-is-expected-to-deliver-on-EU-health-data-space-ePA-and-EUDI-Wallet-11266858.html); [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://www.donneespersonnelles.fr/espace-europeen-donnees-sante); [lesdpodelasante.com](https://www.lesdpodelasante.com/blog/ehds-espace-europeen-donnees-sante-preparation). Web-fetch access to many primary government domains (esante.gouv.fr, gnius.esante.gouv.fr, tehdas.eu, heise.de, ictrecht.nl, en.wikipedia.org, pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) was blocked by this session's network egress proxy; findings below rely on WebSearch-tool synthesis of these and other pages rather than direct verified full-text reads, which is noted where relevant.

Each country section is structured identically for later tabulation: **Existing infrastructure** / **NCPeH & HDAB designation** / **National roadmap & legislation** / **Vendor engagement** / **Gateway/interoperability architecture approach**, followed by Takeaway, Inferences and Gaps.

## Germany

### Takeaway
Germany already has a live opt-out national ePA (since 15 January 2025) on top of the Telematikinfrastruktur (TI), has passed two of the national laws needed for EHDS (DigiG, GDNG) and stood up a secondary-use data body (FDZ Gesundheit) in October 2025, but a third implementing law (GeDIG) is still in progress and full EHDS interconnection is only targeted around 2031.

### Cited Findings
- **Existing infrastructure**: The nationwide ePA ("ePA für alle") has been rolled out since 15 January 2025 and forms the basis for connecting to EHDS; gematik is responsible for the telematics-side technical infrastructure required to connect the German ePA ecosystem to European cross-border exchange services — [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Germany-is-expected-to-deliver-on-EU-health-data-space-ePA-and-EUDI-Wallet-11266858.html).
- German FHIR-based initiatives — ISiK (Informationstechnische Systeme in Krankenhäusern), MIOs (Medizinische Informationsobjekte), and the Medical Informatics Initiative (MII) core dataset — will increasingly need to align with EU EHDS requirements for cross-border exchange and semantic interoperability — [health-samurai.io](https://www.health-samurai.io/articles/fhir-adoption-in-germany).
- **NCPeH/HDAB**: The Forschungsdatenzentrum Gesundheit (FDZ Gesundheit / "Health Data Lab"), established under the Gesundheitsdatennutzungsgesetz (GDNG) and sitting within BfArM (the federal drug-approval agency), publicly launched 9 October 2025 and is described as "well positioned to support Germany's future Health Data Access Body responsibilities under EHDS" — [dsv-europa.de](https://dsv-europa.de/en/news/2025/10/fdz-gesundheit.html); [lw.com (Latham & Watkins)](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/new-health-data-lab-in-germany-facilitates-secondary-use-of-health-data).
- FDZ Health holds fully pseudonymised claims data for statutory-health-insurance members (~90% of the German population), covering 2009–2023 — [lw.com](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/new-health-data-lab-in-germany-facilitates-secondary-use-of-health-data). It "will allow Germany to plug into EHDS once that platform has become fully operational, currently contemplated for 2031" — [lw.com](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/new-health-data-lab-in-germany-facilitates-secondary-use-of-health-data).
- The "Data Access and Coordination Center for Health Data" at BfArM is described as being further developed as part of national EHDS-compliant infrastructure — [görg.de](https://www.goerg.de/de/aktuelles/veroeffentlichungen/09-06-2026/daten-digitalisierung-und-ehds-das-gesundheitswesen-vor-dem-naechsten-grossen-umbruch).
- **National roadmap/legislation**: Two central laws for primary and secondary data use have already passed the Bundestag: the Digital-Gesetz (DigiG) and the Gesundheitsdatennutzungsgesetz (GDNG) — [bundesgesundheitsministerium.de (DigiG)](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/ministerium/gesetze-und-verordnungen/guv-20-lp/digig); [bundesgesundheitsministerium.de (GDNG)](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/ministerium/gesetze-und-verordnungen/guv-20-lp/gesundheitsdatennutzungsgesetz).
- A further law, the "Gesetz für Daten und digitale Innovation im Gesundheitswesen" (GeDIG), was approved by the German Cabinet on 15 July 2026, explicitly framed as continuing implementation of EHDS-compatible data reuse infrastructure — [BMG press release, 15 July 2026](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/kabinett-beschliesst-gedig-pm-15-07-2026).
- The BMG maintains a dedicated EHDS policy page under "Europäische Gesundheitspolitik" — [bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/EHDS](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/themen/internationale-gesundheitspolitik/europa/europaeische-gesundheitspolitik/ehds).
- **Vendor engagement**: A pan-European vendor-readiness market-research study (Black Book Research, industry survey — treat as vendor-market-research, not an EU/government assessment) characterizes Germany's hospital-IT vendor landscape as CompuGroup Medical (CGM), Siemens, Vitagroup and Agfa competing in "sovereignty-driven tenders," with hospitals said to "face the steepest readiness gap" among vendor markets studied; CGM is described as aligning its middleware with gematik ePA standards "extending toward EHDS compliance"; Agfa HealthCare's Orbis Connect product operates in Germany and France; Oracle Health (Cerner) and Epic are described as competing for large academic/enterprise hospital contracts on interoperability depth — [Black Book Research study, via Newswire press release](https://www.newswire.com/news/black-book-research-unveils-first-pan-european-study-of-ehds-22630969) (note: this is a market-research firm's press release, not independently verified by this researcher via direct fetch, and should be weighted as industry commentary).

### Inferences
- Germany's approach is a phased, nationally-legislated build-out (DigiG → GDNG → GeDIG) rather than a single omnibus EHDS transposition law, with the national ePA/TI infrastructure and gematik serving as the technical bridge to MyHealth@EU/EHDS rather than a wholly new parallel system.
- The FDZ Gesundheit is highly likely to become (or heavily inform) Germany's formal HDAB designation, though no source found explicitly states "FDZ Gesundheit IS the designated EHDS Health Data Access Body" — sources use forward-looking/preparatory language ("well positioned to support future HDAB responsibilities").

### Gaps
- No source found with an explicit, dated formal designation announcement naming Germany's HDAB or NCPeH by their EHDS-specific legal title (as opposed to preparatory/adjacent bodies like FDZ Gesundheit and gematik).
- Could not directly verify the Black Book Research findings via primary fetch (newswire.com, heise.de blocked by egress proxy in this session); findings relayed here are as synthesized by the search tool and should be corroborated independently before being treated as fact.

## France

### Takeaway
France starts from a strong base — Mon espace santé (25 million users) and the Health Data Hub — with the Délégation au numérique en santé (DNS) leading transposition work and a national EHDS transposition bill expected in 2026, but formal NCPeH/HDAB designations were not confirmed in available sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Existing infrastructure**: France's existing digital-health assets include Mon Espace Santé with 25 million users, the Health Data Hub, and a digital-health framework that has already imposed FHIR as an exchange standard — [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://www.donneespersonnelles.fr/espace-europeen-donnees-sante).
- The EHDS regulation requires each Member State to stand up a national "Service d'accès aux données de santé électroniques"; in France this role is already partly filled by "Mon espace santé," a digital space launched in 2021 whose implementation is run by the Délégation ministérielle au numérique en santé (DNS) and the Caisse nationale d'assurance maladie (CNAM) — [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://www.donneespersonnelles.fr/espace-europeen-donnees-sante).
- The EHDS regulation adopts an opt-out model similar to Mon Espace Santé, meaning citizens must actively object if they do not want a European medical file created — [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://www.donneespersonnelles.fr/espace-europeen-donnees-sante).
- **NCPeH/HDAB**: The Health Data Hub is positioned as the body responsible for processing secondary-use data access requests (research, innovation, public health, governance) — functionally an HDAB-type role, though no source uses the word "designated" — [WebSearch synthesis of esante.gouv.fr / Health Data Hub site]. A consortium named "French_HealthData_EU," co-financed by the European Commission's Direct Grant and piloted by the Health Data Hub together with 18 French partners, is implementing a metadata catalogue compliant with the European DCAT-AP standard and is on track to conclude by end of 2026 with a roadmap for European cooperation — [health-data-hub.fr](https://www.health-data-hub.fr/actualites/frenchhealth-dataeu-une-etape-cle-vers-la-mise-en-place-de-lespace-europeen-des-donnees).
- **National roadmap/legislation**: EHDS implementation in France requires amending the Code de la santé publique and the "Informatique et Libertés" law, via a bill ("projet de loi") expected in 2026, with work piloted by the DNS — [assemblee-nationale.fr communication document, via WebSearch synthesis](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/content/download/504006/file/Communication%20Espace%20europ%C3%A9en%20donn%C3%A9es%20de%20sant%C3%A9.pdf); the DNS was expected to propose a transposition of the EHDS text "by the end of 2025," with commentary noting "arbitrages devront être faits" (trade-offs will need to be made) and "le temps presse" (time is pressing) — [lesdpodelasante.com](https://www.lesdpodelasante.com/blog/ehds-espace-europeen-donnees-sante-preparation).
- DNS organized a dedicated afternoon session on EHDS on 8 October 2026 to update stakeholders on implementation work — [esante.gouv.fr, per WebSearch synthesis] (direct fetch of esante.gouv.fr was blocked in this session; this fact could not be independently verified beyond the search snippet).
- **Vendor engagement**: No specific evidence was found in this research session of individual French DPI (Dossier Patient Informatisé) hospital-EMR vendors publicly engaging with EHDS preparation; this is a gap (see below), though the DNS's prior imposition of FHIR as a national exchange standard implies vendors already operate under an interoperability mandate that pre-dates and is compatible with EHDS — [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://www.donneespersonnelles.fr/espace-europeen-donnees-sante).
- **Gateway/architecture approach**: France's approach is centrally-led and state/CNAM-run (DNS + CNAM operating Mon espace santé nationally, Health Data Hub centralizing secondary-use access), rather than a hospital/vendor-led model — inferred from the consistent framing of DNS and CNAM as the operators of the national access point — [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://www.donneespersonnelles.fr/espace-europeen-donnees-sante).

### Inferences
- France is likely to extend its existing centralized state infrastructure (Mon espace santé/DNS/CNAM for primary use; Health Data Hub for secondary use) to serve as its EHDS national access services, rather than building parallel new bodies, but the enabling law was not yet passed as of the sources found (expected 2026).

### Gaps
- No explicit, named designation of France's NCPeH could be confirmed (esante.gouv.fr, the most likely primary source, could not be fetched directly in this session due to proxy blocking).
- No confirmation found of a formally designated HDAB by name/legal act (Health Data Hub's role is described functionally/prospectively, not as a confirmed formal designation).
- No evidence found on individual French hospital EMR vendor (DPI) engagement with EHDS specifically.

## Estonia

### Takeaway
Estonia's X-Road-based, federated national EHR (launched 2008, world-first) is frequently cited in EHDS literature as a template for federated/decentralized data exchange, but this research could not confirm specific Estonian EHDS designations (NCPeH, HDAB) or a published national EHDS roadmap document — this is a genuine information gap, not an absence of activity.

### Cited Findings
- X-Road is a decentralized digital data-exchange layer launched in 2001, now facilitating over 2.2 billion transactions annually and powering more than 3,000 digital services spanning e-health, taxation and e-voting — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352340925010753); [cyber.ee](https://cyber.ee/resources/case-studies/estonian-interoperability-framework-x-road/).
- Estonia was the first country to implement a nationwide EHR system registering virtually all residents' medical history from birth to death, using the X-Road digital public-service layer, launched 17 December 2008 — [WebSearch synthesis of Wikipedia/GHDx]; a Lancet Digital Health paper titled "Federated electronic health records for the European Health Data Space" uses Estonia's federated model as a reference point for EHDS-style architecture — [Lancet Digital Health](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landig/article/PIIS2589-7500(23)00156-5/fulltext).
- Estonia and Finland have connected their respective data-exchange layers for cross-border interoperability, with several cross-border public services already running, primarily in healthcare — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352340925010753).
- The Ministry of Social Affairs is in charge of health-data policy and strategy, working alongside the (now-merged) Estonian e-Health Foundation, as the main institutions behind the Estonian National Health Information System — [WebSearch synthesis].
- General EHDS mechanism: National Contact Points for eHealth (NCPeH) act as an "organisational and technical gateway" enabling electronic health-data exchange between countries under MyHealth@EU — [European Commission, via WebSearch synthesis](https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/my-rights-over-my-health-data_en).

### Inferences
- Given Estonia already operates a centralized health information exchange atop X-Road and has run cross-border exchange with Finland, it is plausible Estonia is comparatively well-positioned technically for EHDS NCPeH functions — but this is an inference, not confirmed by an official Estonian designation source in this research.
- The framing of Estonia as an EHDS "reference model" appears in academic/policy literature (e.g., the Lancet Digital Health piece) as an architectural analogy (federated data exchange), not as an official EU statement that Estonia's system is THE reference implementation for EHDS.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm Estonia's designated NCPeH or HDAB by name (e.g., whether Terviseamet/Health Board, TEHIK, or another body holds these roles).
- Could not find a published Estonian national EHDS implementation roadmap or strategy document in this session's searches.
- Could not verify hospital/EMR vendor engagement with EHDS specifically in Estonia (Estonia's e-health ecosystem is unusually state/registry-centric compared to hospital-vendor-centric systems elsewhere, which may make this question less applicable, but this is an inference).

## Netherlands

### Takeaway
The Netherlands is legislating its EHDS transposition now: a first tranche bill, the "Wet op het Gezondheidsinformatiestelsel" (Wgis), just closed public consultation (8 July 2026) and proposes a new single body — the Gezondheidsdata-autoriteit (GDA) — combining the Digital Health Authority, HDAB and National Contact Point for Secondary Use; existing MedMij governance is seen as a partial but incomplete foundation for EHDS citizen rights, and vendor-led/regional interoperability (e.g., ChipSoft–Epic data exchange) continues in parallel.

### Cited Findings
- **Existing infrastructure**: The MedMij Afsprakenstelsel (agreement framework) is assessed as "a promising basis" ("kansrijke basis") for EHDS citizen rights, since it already aligns with rights to access, retrieve and share health data, though "further steps are needed to fully support all rights" — [MedMij.nl](https://medmij.nl/media/medmij-kansrijke-basis-voor-ehds-burgerrechten-verdere-stappen-nodig/); [ICT&health](https://www.icthealth.nl/nieuws/verkenning-medmij-biedt-basis-voor-ehds-burgerrechten).
- A research project running December 2025–June 2026 examined how EHDS citizen rights could be implemented in the Netherlands via a national access service, and what role MedMij could play in it — [MedMij.nl, via WebSearch synthesis].
- On the hospital-vendor side, Tjongerschans hospital (using ChipSoft's HiX Digital Health Services Platform) and MCL (using Epic's "Care Everywhere") became the first hospitals in the Netherlands to exchange patient data between the two EMR platforms directly, using "a universal plug" based on national and international standards, enabling data exchange between personal health environments (PGOs), hospitals, GPs and other providers — [ChipSoft press release](https://chipsoft.com/en/news/539/Pioneering-exchange-of-medical-data-achieved-by-ChipSoft-and-Epic).
- The Dutch hospital EPD/PDMS (patient-data-management-system) market is described as "further consolidating around ChipSoft and Epic" in 2026, with ChipSoft emphasizing standardization, AI and Medical Device Class IIb certification — [M&I/Partners, "EPD-landschap 2026"](https://mxi.nl/kennis/746/epd-landschap-2026-tussen-standaardisatie-en-innovatie).
- **NCPeH/HDAB**: The proposed Wet op het Gezondheidsinformatiestelsel (Wgis) — described as "EHDS tranche 1" — creates a new independent administrative body, the Gezondheidsdata-autoriteit (GDA), which would house the Digital Health Authority (Autoriteit Digitale Gezondheid, ADG/DHA), the Health Data Access Body (HDAB), and the National Contact Point for Secondary Use (NCPSG) all within one organization — [ICTRecht, "Van Wegiz tot Wet GIS"](https://www.ictrecht.nl/blog/van-wegiz-tot-wet-gis-de-nederlandse-invulling-van-de-ehds); [Van Doorne](https://www.vandoorne.com/artikelen/internetconsultatie-wet-op-het-gezondheidsinformatiestelsel-ehds-tranche-1/).
- The HDAB is tasked with assessing applications and issuing licences for secondary use, providing access to health data, and monitoring compliance, and per EHDS must operate completely independently and avoid conflicts of interest — [WebSearch synthesis of Van Doorne/datavoorgezondheid.nl].
- The public consultation ("internetconsultatie") on this first EHDS legislative tranche closed 8 July 2026, with the Ministry of Health, Welfare and Sport (VWS) now reviewing responses — [datavoorgezondheid.nl](https://www.datavoorgezondheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/07/09/internetconsultatie-eerste-ehds-wetgevingstranche-gesloten).
- A news item dated 28 May 2026 describes the introduction of the Gezondheidsdata-autoriteit as "marking a new phase in digital care transformation" — [datavoorgezondheid.nl](https://www.datavoorgezondheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/05/28/introductie-gezondheidsdata-autoriteit-markeert-nieuwe-fase-in-digitale-zorgtransformatie).
- **Vendor engagement/obligations**: For 2026–2027, Dutch guidance states ICT service providers must ensure priority data categories (patient summaries and prescriptions) comply with the European EHR Exchange Format (EEHRxF) and begin self-certification — [ICTRecht, "De EHDS: 5 verplichtingen voor ICT-dienstverleners in de zorg," via WebSearch synthesis](https://www.ictrecht.nl/en/blog/de-ehds-5-verplichtingen-voor-ict-dienstverleners-in-de-zorg).
- The Netherlands must be fully ready for EHDS to become operational by 26 March 2031 at the latest — [ICTRecht, "The implementation of the EHDS: where do we stand in early 2026?," via WebSearch synthesis](https://www.ictrecht.nl/en/blog/de-implementatie-van-de-ehds-waar-staan-we-begin-2026).

### Inferences
- The Netherlands' post-failed-national-EPD model (regional/vendor-led interoperability via MedMij/PGOs, with VZVZ as scheme manager) is being extended rather than replaced: the new Wgis/GDA legislative approach layers EHDS-mandated bodies (HDAB, NCPSG, DHA) on top of, rather than instead of, the existing MedMij consent/exchange framework. Note: this session did not find a primary source directly describing the historical 2011 rejection of the centralized "Landelijk EPD," so this specific historical claim is not independently cited here (see Gaps) even though it is widely known context.
- The ChipSoft–Epic cross-vendor exchange suggests the Netherlands' practical interoperability progress is occurring vendor-to-vendor under national standards, in parallel with (and potentially informing) the top-down EHDS legislative process.

### Gaps
- No primary source was fetched confirming the historical "failed national EPD" narrative (Landelijk EPD rejection) in this research session; this is likely accurate as background context per general knowledge of Dutch e-health history, but could not be verified/cited from search results obtained here.
- The Wgis bill had not passed as of the sources found (still in post-consultation review as of July–September 2026); exact designation dates for the GDA/HDAB/NCPeH were not found.
- Could not confirm whether the Netherlands has a distinct NCPeH (primary use, patient summary/eprescription) separate from the NCPSG (secondary use) named in sources, or how VZVZ's role interacts with the new GDA.

## Austria

### Takeaway
Austria is frequently characterized as "already very well positioned" for EHDS because its established ELGA electronic health record already mandates structured data types, with a hard national milestone (all relevant health data must be in ELGA from 1 January 2026, pathology reports from 1 January 2028) preceding EHDS's own timelines; Austria is actively building its NCPeH and HDAB on top of the existing ELGA infrastructure via the AT-eHDSI and HealthData@AT projects.

### Cited Findings
- **Existing infrastructure**: ELGA (Elektronische Gesundheitsakte) has been Austria's EHR solution since 2015; health data are not stored centrally — only an index/pointer showing that data exist and can be accessed by authorised health service providers is centralized — [gnius.esante.gouv.fr, via WebSearch synthesis](https://gnius.esante.gouv.fr/en/decode-ehealth-internationally/digital-healthcare-austria); [European Commission Digital Building Blocks wiki](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=533365925).
- ELGA is opt-out: patients can opt out entirely or on a per-entry basis, and only health professionals can enter data — [SCOOP4C](https://scoop4c.eu/showcase/elga-electronic-health-records); [EC Digital Building Blocks](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=533365925).
- **EHDS-driven national milestone**: Starting 1 January 2026, all relevant health data must be stored in ELGA; from 1 January 2028 this requirement extends to pathology reports — [Springer Nature, "Nationale Infrastrukturen und European Health Data Space" (Die Radiologie), via WebSearch synthesis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00117-026-01637-z) / [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42380337/).
- "Austria is already very well positioned compared to other European countries" because many health-data types are already structured and recorded in ELGA, providing "a solid starting point for adapting to EHDS requirements" — [Springer Nature (Die Radiologie), via WebSearch synthesis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00117-026-01637-z).
- Projects HealthData@AT and Extended EHR@EU support primary and secondary use of health data; Austria participates in the European eHDSI infrastructure for secure cross-border health-data exchange — [Springer Nature, via WebSearch synthesis]; [Gesundheit Österreich GmbH (GÖG), HealthData@AT](https://goeg.at/healthdata_at_secondary_use).
- **NCPeH/HDAB**: Austria is preparing to establish a Health Data Access Body (HDAB), referred to in German-language sources as "Gesundheitsdatenzugangsstelle," as part of EHDS implementation, with GÖG supporting this through the HealthData@AT project — [GÖG](https://goeg.at/healthdata_at_secondary_use); [GÖG, "Ein Schritt in Richtung EHDS"](https://goeg.at/EU_Gesundheitsdatenraum_EHDS).
- The AT-eHDSI project aims to establish Austria's National Contact Point for eHealth (NCPeH) integrated with the existing ELGA infrastructure; ELGA GmbH is among the bodies contributing expert input to Austria's EHDS implementation — [ÖGPI, "EHDS und ELGA," via WebSearch synthesis](https://www.oegpi.at/ehds-und-elga-warum-pflegeinformatik-jetzt-gesundheitsdaten-mitgestalten-muss/).
- Austria is preparing a national health-data catalogue as one of the "business capabilities" of a future EHDS data-access point — [GÖG/ÖGPI, via WebSearch synthesis].
- **Regulation timeline context**: The EHDS Regulation was negotiated from 2022, adopted in 2024, and entered into force in March 2025 — [Regulations Atlas / Haslinger Nagele law firm summary, via WebSearch synthesis](https://www.haslinger-nagele.com/en/der-ehds-neue-regeln-fuer-gesundheitsdaten-ueberblick-und-ausblick/).

### Inferences
- Austria's approach is explicitly government/ELGA-GmbH-led (a state-mandated central index/pointer registry model), not a hospital-by-hospital or vendor-led approach — the 2026/2028 ELGA data-completeness mandates function as a national on-ramp to EHDS compliance.
- Because ELGA already enforces structured data submission from providers, Austria appears to have a head start relative to countries without an equivalent mandatory national EHR-submission requirement — though this framing originates from a single Austrian-context journal article/press synthesis, not an EU-wide comparative ranking.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the exact planned operational date for Austria's HDAB or NCPeH (only that both are "in preparation"/project-stage via HealthData@AT and AT-eHDSI).
- Could not find direct evidence of individual Austrian hospital-IT/EMR vendor engagement with EHDS (Austria's ELGA architecture may reduce vendor-specific EHDS burden since data-index logic sits at the ELGA-GmbH layer rather than the hospital EMR layer, but this is an inference, not a confirmed finding).

## Denmark

### Takeaway
Denmark already operates a live, EU-recognized NCPeH implementation (via the Danish Health Data Authority/Sundhedsdatastyrelsen, publicly documented on GitHub) built on mature national infrastructure (sundhed.dk, MedCom, Det Fælles Medicinkort), and is now undergoing a major governance consolidation — merging sundhed.dk, the Danish Health Data Authority, MedCom and the National Genome Center into a single new body, "Digital Health Denmark," effective 1 January 2027 — explicitly to strengthen coherence for digital health development including EHDS.

### Cited Findings
- **Existing infrastructure**: Sundhed.dk is Denmark's national eHealth portal/access point, described in the 2018 national strategy as "a national access point for personal health-related data for hospitals, general practitioners and communities"; all primary-care doctors have used electronic medical records and been mandated to use computerized record/communication systems since 2004 — [gnius.esante.gouv.fr, via WebSearch synthesis](https://gnius.esante.gouv.fr/en/international-e-health/denmark); [World Health Systems Facts, via WebSearch synthesis](https://healthsystemsfacts.org/denmark-health-system-facts/denmark-health-information-and-communication-technologies/).
- Det Fælles Medicinkort (FMK, the shared national medication record) collects and stores all Danish citizens' medication information in a central database housed at Sundhedsdatastyrelsen; GPs, dentists, specialists and hospital doctors can all update it, and it has been rolled out across all GPs, specialists and municipal elderly care — [MedCom](https://www.medcom.dk/projekter/faelles-medicinkort-fmk); [MedCom PDF, "En fremtid med adgang til et fælles og samlet overblik"](https://medcom.dk/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/en-fremtid-med-adgang-til-et-faelles-og-samlet-overblik-over-en-borgers-aktuelle-medicinering.pdf).
- FMK was developed and implemented by Sundhedsdatastyrelsen in collaboration with Danish Regions, the Danish Medical Association, Local Government Denmark (KL), the Ministry of Health and Social Affairs, the Danish Health Authority, and MedCom — [MedCom](https://www.medcom.dk/projekter/faelles-medicinkort-fmk).
- MedCom is represented in FMK's governance structure and participates in the certification team that tests new/existing vendor systems for FMK integration whenever new versions or interfaces are released — [MedCom](https://www.medcom.dk/projekter/faelles-medicinkort-fmk).
- MedCom acts as a technical-expertise "affiliated partner" to help ensure compliance with EHDS and to support reuse of existing national infrastructure components — [MedCom, "International activities," via WebSearch synthesis](https://medcom.dk/medcom-in-english/international-activities/).
- **NCPeH/HDAB**: Denmark's National Contact Point for eHealth (NCPeH), enabling cross-border medical-information exchange under MyHealth@EU, is maintained by Sundhedsdatastyrelsen (the Danish Health Data Authority); this is documented in a public GitHub repository explicitly titled "Danish implementation of MyHealth @ EU – eHealth Digital Service Infrastructure (eHDSI)" — [GitHub: Sundhedsdatastyrelsen/ehdsi](https://github.com/Sundhedsdatastyrelsen/ehdsi); [English Sundhedsdatastyrelsen, "European Health Data Space"](https://english.sundhedsdatastyrelsen.dk/health-data-and-registers/european-health-data-space).
- No source found explicitly names Denmark's designated HDAB by title (see Gaps); it is plausible this function currently sits within, or is planned to move into, the new Digital Health Denmark body, but this is inference, not confirmed.
- **National roadmap/reorganization**: A new organization, "Digital Health Denmark," will be established 1 January 2027, jointly owned by municipalities, regions and the state, explicitly to strengthen digital development and coherence across the health sector; it will merge sundhed.dk, the Danish Health Data Authority, MedCom, the National Genome Centre, and a number of regionally-managed national IT solutions — [English Sundhedsdatastyrelsen, "Digital Health Denmark"](https://english.sundhedsdatastyrelsen.dk/about-us/digital-health-denmark); [MTR Consult, "Establishing Digital Health Denmark"](https://mtrconsult.com/news/establishing-digital-health-denmark).
- Sundhed.dk, the Danish Health Data Authority and MedCom will cease to exist as independent organizations on 31 December 2026, though certain selected functions/regulatory responsibilities of the Danish Health Data Authority remain within the Ministry of the Interior and Health — [English Sundhedsdatastyrelsen, "Digital Health Denmark"](https://english.sundhedsdatastyrelsen.dk/about-us/digital-health-denmark). A preparatory phase for this merger is ongoing in 2026, with full establishment planned for 1 January 2027 — [WebSearch synthesis of the same source].

### Inferences
- Denmark's approach is unambiguously a state-run, single-infrastructure-owner model: rather than leaving interoperability to hospitals or EMR vendors, Denmark is consolidating even its existing multiple national digital-health bodies (sundhed.dk, MedCom, Danish Health Data Authority, National Genome Centre) into one organization, which strongly suggests EHDS-related national gateway functions (NCPeH, likely HDAB) will be centralized within Digital Health Denmark going forward.
- Denmark appears to be among the more institutionally mature countries for EHDS given it already operates a working, EU-documented NCPeH/eHDSI implementation years ahead of the 2027 application deadline.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm Denmark's designated HDAB by name/legal act.
- Could not find a specific, named Danish EHDS national strategy/roadmap document (only the broader Digital Health Denmark reorganization announcement, which references EHDS-relevant consolidation but is not itself branded as an "EHDS roadmap").
- No hospital/EMR-vendor-specific engagement evidence found for Denmark in this session.

## Sweden

### Takeaway
Sweden's government tasked its national eHealth Agency (2023) with proposing a roadmap for a state-led national digital health infrastructure explicitly to meet EHDS requirements, and has since split secondary-use responsibilities across several authorities (Socialstyrelsen, Statistics Sweden, IVO, eHealth Agency) under the "SENASH" project — but Sweden must also shift from its traditional opt-in consent model to the EU-mandated opt-out model, which analysts flag as a major structural change.

### Cited Findings
- In June 2023 the Swedish government tasked the Swedish eHealth Agency (E-hälsomyndigheten) with developing a roadmap proposal for a national digital infrastructure for health, medical and dental care in which the state takes greater responsibility, and the agency is tasked with coming up with a plan for digital infrastructure to comply with EHDS — [eurohealthobservatory.who.int, WHO Health Systems and Policy Monitor, via WebSearch synthesis](https://eurohealthobservatory.who.int/monitors/health-systems-monitor/updates/hspm/sweden-2023/a-first-step-towards-a-national-digital-infrastructure-for-healthcare).
- The SENASH project on secondary use of health data began in Sweden in 2024, with responsibilities divided among authorities: the National Board of Health and Welfare (Socialstyrelsen) is preparing to handle requests and grant access to health datasets (an HDAB-type function); Statistics Sweden (SCB) is exploring secure processing environments; the Health and Social Care Inspectorate (IVO) will monitor compliance; and the eHealth Agency is facilitating implementation of the national e-health infrastructure — [Gart Solutions, "Sweden HealthTech Analysis 2025–2026," via WebSearch synthesis](https://gartsolutions.com/sweden-healthtech-industry/) (industry/consultancy source; treat as analyst commentary rather than an official government statement).
- The same industry analysis estimates EHDS-related investment needs of €150–400 million by 2028 for Sweden and describes a required shift "from Sweden's traditional opt-in consent model to an EU-mandated opt-out approach" — [Gart Solutions, via WebSearch synthesis](https://gartsolutions.com/sweden-healthtech-industry/) (flagged explicitly as an analyst/consultancy estimate, not an official government-published figure).
- A separate national research initiative, "DIGIfor1healthSE," exists and publishes outputs related to Swedish digital-health infrastructure, though this session did not review its content in depth — [digifor1health.se](https://digifor1health.se/outputs_en/).

### Inferences
- Sweden is pursuing a state-led/centralizing model (echoing Denmark's and Austria's approach) rather than a vendor/hospital-led one, consistent with the government's explicit 2023 mandate that "the state takes greater responsibility" for national digital health infrastructure.
- The opt-in-to-opt-out consent shift required by EHDS is likely to be one of Sweden's more significant legal/political implementation challenges, though this inference is drawn from a single consultancy source and not corroborated by an official Swedish government statement in this research.

### Gaps
- No confirmation found of Sweden's designated NCPeH.
- The €150–400 million investment figure and opt-in/opt-out framing come from a single industry consultancy source (Gart Solutions) not cross-verified against an official Swedish government or EU document in this session.
- No hospital/EMR-vendor-specific engagement evidence found for Sweden.

## Italy

### Takeaway
Italy is explicitly framed (in Italian legal/consultancy commentary) as architecturally aligned with EHDS via its FSE 2.0 national EHR and its new "Gateway" component built on the INI (national interoperability infrastructure) federated model, but persistent regional fragmentation — some regional gateways still not activated as of mid-2026 — is blocking practical data flow even from providers that are technically ready, and Italy is still in the process of designing its HDAB.

### Cited Findings
- The Fascicolo Sanitario Elettronico (FSE), Italy's national electronic health record, originated with a 2012 decree-law and serves as a single access point to health and social-health data and clinical documents from both the National Health Service and private health facilities — [Agenda Digitale](https://www.agendadigitale.eu/sanita/fascicolo-sanitario-elettronico-2-0-il-dato-sanitario-diventa-infrastruttura/); [Fascicolo Sanitario Elettronico official portal](https://www.fascicolosanitario.gov.it/portale/en/home).
- FSE 2.0 introduced a "Gateway" component (the Sogei FSE Gateway, per the Decree of 7 September 2023) forming the operational architecture connecting regional FSE systems; legal/consultancy commentary describes this primary-use architecture as "structurally compatible with EHDS" — [noze.it, "Gateway FSE e Decreto 7 settembre 2023"](https://www.noze.it/insights/fse-2-0-gateway-sogei/).
- Italy's federated model, based on the INI (national interoperability infrastructure) plus the FSE 2.0 Gateway, is described as "mirror[ing] the EHDS vision of a decentralized yet interconnected ecosystem, where national systems remain sovereign but are seamlessly integrated into a European-level infrastructure" — [noze.it, via WebSearch synthesis]; similar framing appears in a peer-reviewed article, "The challenges of national health data ecosystems in feeding the European health data space: the Italian example" — [Frontiers in Medicine / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12719427/).
- Historically, Italy's FSE system faced ~20 different regional implementations, with interoperability problems between regions and hospitals, and a lack of automatic data upload from private clinics undermining effectiveness — [PMC, "The Italian health data system is broken" (editorial) and its response](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11980697/); [PMC response article](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11927704/).
- As of May 2026, private healthcare providers were producing FSE 2.0-compliant digital data, but non-activation of regional gateways was still blocking access to that data — per a market-research vendor survey ("Black Book Italy Provider Pulse") reported by a regional Italian outlet — [Maremma News, citing Black Book Italy Provider Pulse](https://maremmanews.it/salute/2026-05-03/fse-2-0-la-sanita-privata-produce-dati-digitali-ma-la-mancata-attivazione-dei-gateway-regionali-blocca-l-accesso) (note: this is a regional news outlet relaying a market-research firm's press release, not a primary government source, and should be weighted accordingly).
- **HDAB**: Italy is still actively designing its HDAB; a legal/consultancy readiness checklist states the public-administration task is "designing the Italian HDAB, updating the national regulatory frame, dialogue with European counterparts," and that HDAB implementation requires integration with the data-protection authority (Garante), Ethics Committees, the medicines agency (AIFA), the national agency for regional health services (Agenas), and the national health institute (ISS) — [noze.it, "EHDS verso il 2027: HDAB, data permit e secondary use"](https://www.noze.it/insights/ehds-secondary-use-2027/).
- Agenas (Agenzia nazionale per i servizi sanitari regionali) hosts national strategic-project information on the FSE on its own site — [Agenas](https://www.agenas.gov.it/agenzia-per-la-sanit%C3%A0-digitale/progetti-strategici/fse).

### Inferences
- Italy exemplifies a hybrid gateway model: a national interoperability layer (INI + FSE 2.0 Gateway) is mandated top-down, but activation and compliance still depend heavily on regional health authorities (Italy's regions run their own health systems), meaning the "national gateway vs. left-to-regions/hospitals" dichotomy in Italy is really a spectrum — national architecture exists, but regional execution lags.
- Italy's HDAB is not yet operational as of the sources found (design/coordination phase), suggesting Italy may be positioned in the "behind" group for secondary-use readiness relative to countries like Austria or Denmark with more mature single-body governance, though this is this researcher's inference, not an explicit EU ranking.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm which specific body will be formally designated as Italy's NCPeH (the INI/Gateway architecture suggests a national-level candidate, but no explicit designation was found).
- The regional-gateway-activation problem is sourced from a single regional news outlet relaying an industry market-research press release; this should be corroborated with an official Italian Ministry of Health or Agenas source before being treated as confirmed fact.

## Spain

### Takeaway
Spain's existing Historia Clínica Digital del Sistema Nacional de Salud (HCDSNS) links health records across its 17 devolved regional health systems, and the Ministerio de Sanidad runs a dedicated "Espacio Europeo de Datos de Salud" webpage, but this research could not confirm a formal HDAB or NCPeH designation for Spain — available commentary describes the expected HDAB structure as "presumably" involving the Ministry of Health and the autonomous communities, explicitly flagged by the source itself as not yet confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Spain's national tools for health-information sharing include the Individual Health Card (Tarjeta Sanitaria Individual), the Historia Clínica Digital del Sistema Nacional de Salud (HCDSNS), and the Electronic Prescription of the SNS (Receta Electrónica) — [Ministerio de Sanidad](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/en/profesionales/hcdsns/home.htm).
- The Ministerio de Sanidad's "Salud Digital" area includes a specific section titled "Espacio Europeo de Datos de Salud" (European Health Data Space) — [Ministerio de Sanidad](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/espacioEuropeoDS/home.htm); and "Historia Clínica Electrónica en la UE" — [Ministerio de Sanidad](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/historiaClinicaUE/home.htm).
- Regulation (EU) 2025/327 was approved 11 February 2025, published in the DOUE on 5 March 2025, and in force since 26 March 2025, with full application phased between 2026 and 2029 — [saludcomply.com, "Que es el EHDS," via WebSearch synthesis](https://saludcomply.com/guia/que-es-ehds) (a private consultancy/blog guide, not an official government source, but the regulatory dates align with other sources cited above).
- Spain's HDAB "will presumably be structured through the Ministry of Health with participation from autonomous communities," with the HDAB expected to issue data permits, audit compliance, and impose sanctions — [WebSearch synthesis, drawing on saludcomply.com and dondeestalasaluddigital.com] (explicitly hedged as "presumably" by the underlying source itself — i.e., this is analyst speculation about the likely structure, not a confirmed government designation).

### Inferences
- Given Spain's health system is organized around 17 autonomous communities each running separate regional health services (as in Italy), any Spanish HDAB/NCPeH is likely to require the same kind of central-government/regional coordination mechanism seen in Italy's INI/Gateway model — but no Spanish-specific architecture document was found to confirm this.

### Gaps

- No published Spanish national EHDS implementation roadmap or transposition law found in this session's searches.
- No hospital/EMR-vendor-specific engagement evidence found for Spain.

## Cross-Country Synthesis: Readiness Rankings and Gateway-vs-Vendor Architecture

### Takeaway
Multiple independent assessments (an EU Joint Action, an industry advisory firm, and a market-research vendor-survey company) agree that EHDS readiness is highly uneven across the EU and that no Member State was "fully ready" as of the assessments found; separately, a consistent architectural pattern is described across sources: countries are increasingly building a dedicated national interoperability "gateway" layer between hospital/vendor systems and the EU's MyHealth@EU/HealthData@EU infrastructure, rather than leaving cross-border and secondary-use compliance to individual hospitals or EMR vendors to solve unassisted.

### Cited Findings
- **TEHDAS Joint Action** ("Towards the European Health Data Space," an EU co-funded joint action) found "a heterogeneous picture" in countries' readiness to comply with EHDS provisions, concluding "no member state is fully ready yet to comply with the future regulation"; political will was strong across Member States, but there is a "significant need for more staff with technical and legal expertise and more time" — [TEHDAS, "Member states' readiness to benefit from the EHDS regulation varies," via WebSearch synthesis](https://tehdas.eu/tehdas1/results/member-states-readiness-to-benefit-from-the-ehds-regulation-varies/); the underlying peer-reviewed study is "Are EU member states ready for the European Health Data Space? Lessons learnt on the secondary use of health data from the TEHDAS Joint Action" — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39514641); [Oxford Academic, European Journal of Public Health](https://academic.oup.com/eurpub/article/34/6/1102/7887708). (Note: direct fetch of tehdas.eu was blocked by this session's egress proxy; the summary above is drawn from WebSearch-tool synthesis of the page and its abstract, not a verified full-text read.)
- **Better (better.care) 2026 "EHDS Implementation Readiness Report"**, based on interviews with national health officials, programme leads, technical architects and clinical informaticists across Europe, found that "while its goals are broadly understood and strongly supported, many health systems are struggling to move from policy ambition to operational reality," that "national readiness varies widely, governance models remain unclear," and that "the decisive barriers are rarely the standards themselves" — instead citing "fragmented governance, inconsistent semantic quality, legacy infrastructure, and limited implementation capacity" as the real pressure points; stakeholder interviews surfaced "three distinct implementation realities depending on the maturity of the environment" — [Better, "European Health Data Space (EHDS): Stakeholder perspectives on implementation readiness"](https://www.better.care/document/ehds-implementation-readiness-report/); [Better blog, "EHDS implementation: From policy to practice"](https://www.better.care/blog-en/ehds-implementation-policy-practice/).
- **Black Book Research** (a healthcare-IT market-research firm) published a 2026 pan-European vendor-readiness study finding that "only 13% of Healthcare IT Users say vendors are EHDS-ready," under the headline "Europe is Connected, But Not Yet Interoperable" — [Newswire, Black Book Research press release](https://www.newswire.com/news/black-book-study-finds-europe-is-connected-but-not-yet-interoperable-22799022); a related release, "EHDS Readiness in Europe: Black Book Country Vendor Rankings," implies the firm produced country-by-country vendor rankings, though this session could not fetch its full content (egress-blocked) to extract which specific countries ranked where — [AccessNewswire](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/healthcare-and-pharmaceutical/black-book-study-finds-europe-is-connected-but-not-yet-interoperable-1177035). (This is a commercial market-research firm's self-published survey, not an EU institutional or peer-reviewed source, and should be weighted accordingly — treat the 13% figure and any implied country rankings as industry-survey data pending independent corroboration.)
- Ireland's national audit body, HIQA, published a report specifically assessing Ireland's readiness for EHDS — cited here only to note that at least one Member State (Ireland, not in this brief's core scope) has a formal government-commissioned national readiness assessment, illustrating that such assessments are becoming a recognized national governance step — [HIQA](https://www.hiqa.ie/hiqa-news-updates/new-hiqa-report-assesses-irelands-readiness-european-health-data-space-regulation).
- A ScienceDirect article specifically titled "Is Poland ready for the European health data space?" indicates comparable national readiness-assessment literature exists for other Member States beyond this brief's core scope — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211883726000158).
- **Gateway/central-platform architecture**: The European Commission launched "HealthData@EU," a central platform released as open source in March 2025, providing "a catalogue, gateway services, and interoperability components for the secondary use of health data across Member States" — [Better, "EHDS infrastructure: platforms powering EU health data"](https://www.better.care/blog-en/ehds-infrastructure-platforms/).
- The same source describes an emerging pattern across Europe: "a consistent architectural pattern is emerging: national EHDS infrastructure layers that sit between clinical systems and the European MyHealth@EU infrastructure," arguing this "national layer absorbs the complexity that EHR vendors cannot realistically handle on their own — ensuring semantic alignment, data quality, consent enforcement, IPS generation, and full auditability before anything crosses a border" — [Better, "EHDS infrastructure: platforms powering EU health data"](https://www.better.care/blog-en/ehds-infrastructure-platforms/) (industry-vendor commentary; treat as a description of a trend/argument rather than an official EU architectural mandate).
- Italy's FSE 2.0 Gateway plus INI federated model is explicitly cited as an example of this pattern: "a federated model...mirrors the EHDS vision of a decentralized yet interconnected ecosystem, where national systems remain sovereign but are seamlessly integrated into a European-level infrastructure" — [noze.it](https://www.noze.it/insights/fse-2-0-gateway-sogei/).

### Inferences
- Synthesizing the country sections above, the countries showing the clearest evidence of a *government-built, centrally-run national gateway/interoperability layer* (rather than leaving integration to individual hospitals or vendors) are: **Austria** (ELGA/ELGA GmbH as central index-and-access layer, plus AT-eHDSI), **Denmark** (Sundhedsdatastyrelsen/soon-to-be Digital Health Denmark as sole national infrastructure owner), **Estonia** (X-Road as a foundational, state-run federated exchange layer), **France** (DNS/CNAM-run Mon espace santé + centralized Health Data Hub), and **Italy** (INI + FSE 2.0 national Gateway, notwithstanding regional execution gaps). **Germany** and the **Netherlands** show more of a hybrid pattern — national legal/institutional scaffolding (FDZ Gesundheit/gematik in Germany; the proposed GDA in the Netherlands) is being built specifically because interoperability has historically been more vendor- and region-fragmented (multiple competing hospital-IT vendors in Germany; MedMij/PGO ecosystem plus direct vendor-to-vendor deals like ChipSoft–Epic in the Netherlands).
- On "ahead vs. behind": Austria and Denmark appear, from the sources found, to be comparatively advanced on institutional/infrastructural readiness (existing national EHR/index systems, explicit near-term legal mandates already in force or imminent). Italy and Spain show more visible structural/regional fragmentation risk (regional gateway activation problems in Italy; devolved regional health systems in Spain with no confirmed national HDAB yet). Germany and France are mid-position: strong existing national platforms (ePA/TI; Mon espace santé/Health Data Hub) but implementing legislation and formal body designations still incomplete or in progress as of the sources found. This ranking is this researcher's synthesis across multiple partial sources, not a single authoritative EU ranking — no source found provided an explicit ordinal ranking of these nine countries.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative EU-level document (e.g., an official European Commission or eHealth Network country-by-country EHDS readiness scorecard) was found or fetched in this session (health.ec.europa.eu EHDS pages appeared in search results but were not independently fetched/verified beyond snippets, and several targeted EU-institutional URLs were not directly tested). A dedicated follow-up search of health.ec.europa.eu's "EHDS in action" and eHealth Network materials, and direct outreach to TEHDAS's published deliverables, would likely close this gap.
- The Black Book Research country-vendor-ranking release was identified but not fetched; its specific per-country rankings remain unknown from this research.
- This session's WebFetch tool was blocked by the network egress proxy for essentially all government (.gouv.fr, .dk, .gv.at equivalents were not tested directly but similar patterns seen), EU-institutional, and reference domains (Wikipedia, PubMed) attempted; all findings above rely on the WebSearch tool's own synthesis of source content rather than this researcher's direct verification of full page text. Where a finding matters significantly for downstream decisions, it should be re-verified via direct access before being treated as certain.
