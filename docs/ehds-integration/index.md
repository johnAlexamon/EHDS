# EHDS integration for HIS/EMR systems: overview

This folder documents what the EU's European Health Data Space Regulation — [Regulation (EU) 2025/327](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R0327) — requires of Hospital Information Systems (HIS), Electronic Medical Record (EMR) and Electronic Health Record (EHR) vendors and the healthcare providers who run them, with a specific focus on Finland and a comparative view of eight other EU member states. It also assesses whether a commercial market exists for EHDS gateway/integration components, who is likely to build and operate those gateways country by country, and whether the underlying technical APIs (the European Electronic Health Record Exchange Format, or EEHRxF) are settled or still being specified. The Regulation entered into force on 26 March 2025 and phases in obligations through 2027, 2029, 2031 and 2035; as of this research (September 2026), most of the governance bodies are being stood up but the core technical specifications are not yet finalized, which is the central fact shaping the market and integration picture described here.

## Methodology & confidence notes

This documentation set was synthesized from five research notes compiled in a prior research pass. **Direct web-page retrieval (WebFetch) was blocked by the research environment's network egress proxy for nearly all primary sources**, including eur-lex.europa.eu, the European Commission's health pages, national government sites (stm.fi, thl.fi, kanta.fi, findata.fi, valtioneuvosto.fi, and equivalents in other member states), and most law-firm/industry analysis sites. As a result:

- **Nearly all findings in this documentation set are based on search-engine-synthesized summaries of source pages, not on independently verified, directly-fetched primary text.** Search snippets can misquote, over-simplify, or misattribute article numbers.
- **Exact article numbers, Annex content, and precise legal wording of Regulation (EU) 2025/327 should be independently verified against [EUR-Lex CELEX:32025R0327](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R0327) before being relied on for any legal or compliance decision.** Several article numbers cited in these files were explicitly flagged as unverified in the underlying research and are marked with a ⚠️ callout where they appear.
- Some facts conflict between sources (for example, the exact entry-into-force date is reported as both 25 and 26 March 2025 by different sources). These conflicts are called out explicitly in the relevant file rather than silently resolved.
- Several market-size and vendor-activity claims (e.g., "€7 billion by 2027" for EHDS middleware, vendor rankings) trace back to a single market-research firm's press releases (Black Book Research) that could not be independently fetched and verified. These are labeled as industry commentary/analyst speculation, not confirmed fact.
- Where a claim is described in the underlying research as unconfirmed, contradictory, or speculative, this documentation preserves that caveat visibly (via inline "⚠️ Unverified / conflicting sources" notes) rather than smoothing it into a confident-sounding statement.
- Today's date for context: this synthesis was produced in September 2026, roughly six months before the Regulation's March 2027 general-application milestone.

## Executive summary

- **What EHR/HIS systems must do, and by when:** EHDS creates a self-certification, CE-marking-style conformity regime (Chapter III) for EHR systems handling defined "priority categories" of health data, plus operational obligations on healthcare providers under primary use (Chapter II, patient access and cross-border exchange via MyHealth@EU) and secondary use (Chapter IV, data sharing with national Health Data Access Bodies via HealthData@EU). Obligations phase in on a **2027 → 2029 → 2031 → 2035** timeline, with 26 March 2029 covering patient summaries and ePrescriptions, and 26 March 2031 covering medical imaging, lab results and discharge reports. See [01-legal-and-technical-framework.md](01-legal-and-technical-framework.md) and [02-implementation-timeline.md](02-implementation-timeline.md).
- **Finland's position:** Finland is one of the most EHDS-ready member states, thanks to its mature Kanta infrastructure (operated by Kela), an existing 2019 Secondary Use Act and data-permit authority (Findata), and years of live cross-border MyHealth@EU participation. Finnish HIS vendors (TietoEvry/Lifecare, CGI/OMNI360, Mediconsult) and the Epic-based Apotti system will need to adapt to EEHRxF/FHIR-based interfaces, but no vendor has published a public EHDS technical roadmap as of the underlying research. See [03-finland.md](03-finland.md).
- **How other member states compare:** Readiness is highly uneven. Austria, Denmark, Estonia, France and Italy show clearer centralized-gateway models (built on ELGA, Sundhedsdatastyrelsen, X-Road, Mon espace santé/Health Data Hub, and the FSE 2.0/INI Gateway respectively); Germany and the Netherlands show more hybrid, vendor/region-fragmented patterns now being consolidated by new legal bodies (FDZ Gesundheit, the proposed Gezondheidsdata-autoriteit). No member state was found to be "fully ready" as of the sources reviewed. See [04-other-member-states.md](04-other-member-states.md).
- **Is there a market for gateway/integration components, and who builds them?** MyHealth@EU is architected as a **national-monopoly gateway per country** (one National Contact Point for eHealth, or NCPeH) rather than a competitive multi-vendor layer — but the technology stack behind each national gateway is typically procured from vendors via national tender. Evidence (concentrated in one market-research firm's releases, so treat cautiously) points to incumbent health-IT vendors with existing EU public-sector footprints — Dedalus, InterSystems, CompuGroup Medical — building EHDS-branded middleware, with openEHR-native challengers (Better, Marand) also active in pilots. See [05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md](05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md).
- **Are the technical APIs decided?** No, but concrete drafts exist. The EEHRxF is explicitly described as "not yet formally established," but HL7 Europe and IHE Europe's joint **EURIDICE** initiative has published real, inspectable draft FHIR Implementation Guides for every EEHRxF priority category plus a transport/API layer (the "EU Health Data API," built on IHE MHD/PDQm, HL7 IPA and SMART Backend Services), and a manifest-based imaging profile (**MADO**) layered on top of DICOMweb. Two of these reached a formal STU1 ballot in March–April 2026; the rest remain unballoted CI-builds. All of it is ahead of a statutory 26 March 2027 deadline for the European Commission to adopt binding "common specifications" via implementing acts, so building a production connector today carries meaningful rework risk. See [05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md](05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md) and, for the actual draft specs and real examples, [06-api-drafts-and-specifications.md](06-api-drafts-and-specifications.md).
- **Will HIS/EMR vendors have to implement the EHDS API directly?** It's a per-country choice, explicitly left open by the spec itself. The draft "EU Health Data API" defines two named national architecture patterns: a **centralized-repository** model where the national gateway does all the querying and the vendor only needs to *publish* data to it (named as typical for existing national document-repository countries), and a **federated-query** model where each vendor's own EHR must host a live query API directly (named explicitly for the Netherlands and Sweden). Cross-border country-to-country exchange is a separate, third layer again, run NCP-to-NCP. See [06-api-drafts-and-specifications.md](06-api-drafts-and-specifications.md) for the source quotes and a diagram of a two-country cross-border pull.
