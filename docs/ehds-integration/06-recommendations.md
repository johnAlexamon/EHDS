# Recommendations for a prospective EHDS gateway/integration vendor

This file translates the findings in the preceding five files into practical guidance for someone evaluating whether to build an EHDS gateway/integration product. It is analysis and synthesis built on the sourced facts documented elsewhere in this folder — it does not introduce new factual claims beyond what is cited in [01](01-legal-and-technical-framework.md)–[05](05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md). Treat this file as a strategic opinion informed by the evidence, not as independently verified fact, and re-read the "Methodology & confidence notes" in the [README](README.md) before acting on it.

## Market timing view

The core tension for a prospective vendor is that **the specification target is not yet stable**, but **the procurement cycle that will pick winners is already starting**. As documented in [05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md](05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md), the EEHRxF is explicitly described as "not yet formally established," with the Commission's binding "common specifications" not due until 26 March 2027 (per Article 36), and even that date is only the start of a further implementation/compliance period. At the same time, national governance bodies (HDABs, Digital Health Authorities, NCPeH operators) are being stood up now — the EHDS Board was established by implementing act in April 2026, MyHealth@EU and HealthDCAT-AP implementing regulations followed in September/October 2026 (see [02-implementation-timeline.md](02-implementation-timeline.md)) — and several countries' national procurement/legislative processes (Germany's GeDIG, the Netherlands' Wgis/GDA, France's expected 2026 transposition bill) are moving through 2026 (see [04-other-member-states.md](04-other-member-states.md)).

This means: **2025–2027 is a spec-risk window but also a relationship- and incumbency-building window.** Vendors who engage now (Dedalus, InterSystems, Better, Marand, per the vendor activity documented in [05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md](05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md) — noting that evidence is concentrated in one market-research firm's releases and should be treated cautiously) are accepting real rework risk in exchange for early influence over the eventual spec and a head start on the national tenders that will matter most. A vendor entering after 2027, once common specifications are adopted, faces lower technical risk but is competing against incumbents who may already hold the relevant national contracts.

## Which countries look most promising first, and why

Based on the comparative picture in [03-finland.md](03-finland.md) and [04-other-member-states.md](04-other-member-states.md):

- **Finland** is a strong near-term target for a vendor with a working EHDS/FHIR-capable interoperability product: it has mature, mandatory national infrastructure (Kanta), an existing secondary-use authority with a live technical-modernization project (Findata's FinHITS, 2023–2027), and a concentrated vendor market (essentially three HIS vendors — TietoEvry, CGI, Mediconsult — plus Apotti/Epic). No incumbent has yet published a public EHDS technical roadmap, which is itself an opening. The caution is that Kanta's own development budget is reported as shrinking in 2026, which could slow the pace of tender activity even if the policy commitment is strong.
- **Austria and Denmark** show the clearest evidence of a centrally-run, single-infrastructure-owner gateway model already in motion (ELGA/ELGA GmbH plus AT-eHDSI in Austria; Sundhedsdatastyrelsen consolidating into "Digital Health Denmark" from 1 January 2027). A single national buyer with a near-term legal mandate is a cleaner sales target than a fragmented, multi-buyer market — but it also means the field for "who gets to be the technology behind the gateway" narrows fast once that buyer picks a supplier.
- **Estonia and France** likewise show centralized-gateway patterns (X-Road; DNS/CNAM plus Health Data Hub) with existing large-scale citizen-facing platforms, suggesting the technology decision may already lean toward extending existing state-run infrastructure rather than opening to new entrants — worth engaging as a subcontractor/technology partner to the incumbent state operator rather than expecting to win the "gateway" contract outright.
- **Germany and the Netherlands** are more fragmented markets in transition (Germany's competing hospital-IT vendors — CGM, Siemens, Vitagroup, Agfa — under new GDNG/GeDIG legal scaffolding; the Netherlands' MedMij/PGO ecosystem being layered under a new GDA). Fragmentation plus new legal bodies being stood up now is exactly the kind of market where a specialized integration vendor can find a niche, but it is also where large incumbents with existing gematik/MedMij relationships have the inside track.
- **Italy and Spain** carry the most execution risk in the near term: Italy's FSE 2.0/INI Gateway architecture exists on paper, but regional gateway activation is reportedly lagging as of mid-2026, and its HDAB is still in design; Spain's HDAB/NCPeH structure is unconfirmed and only "presumed" by analysts. These may be longer-horizon opportunities once national/regional coordination matures, rather than near-term targets.

## Key risks

1. **Spec instability until ~2027 (and possibly later).** Building a production connector against HL7 Europe's current Standard-for-Trial-Use FHIR Implementation Guides, or against the EURIDICE joint specifications, means building against material "explicitly in trial-use/draft status" that is likely to change before the Commission's common specifications are legally finalized (see [05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md](05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md)). Even after March 2027, the "common template and implementation time limit" language suggests a further transition period before mandatory conformance — full market-wide stability may not arrive until 2028 or later.
2. **Fragmented national procurement.** MyHealth@EU is architected as one national-monopoly gateway per country (the NCPeH), not a competitive multi-vendor layer — so a vendor's addressable market is a series of separate national (and in Italy's and Spain's case, sub-national/regional) procurement processes, each with its own timeline, incumbent relationships, and legal framework, rather than one EU-wide sale.
3. **Incumbent vendors building in-house.** Established EU-facing health-IT vendors with existing public-sector footprints (Dedalus, InterSystems, CompuGroup Medical) appear to be building or re-architecting their own middleware for EHDS rather than relying on third-party integration engines (see [05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md](05-gateway-market-and-api-status.md)). A new entrant competing head-on with these incumbents on their home turf (e.g., Dedalus in Italy) faces a steep climb; a partnership or subcontractor model, or a focus on markets/niches where incumbents are weaker (e.g., openEHR-native positioning where Better/Marand are reportedly active), may be more tractable.
4. **Market-size estimates are unreliable.** The one specific EHDS-middleware market figure found ("€7 billion by 2027") comes from a single market-research firm's press release that could not be independently verified, and general healthcare-interoperability market forecasts vary widely ($4–13B depending on source and scope). Financial planning should not rely on either figure without independent validation.
5. **Uneven Member State readiness creates uneven, unpredictable timing.** Multiple independent assessments (TEHDAS, Better's readiness report, Black Book Research) agree no member state is "fully ready," and readiness varies enormously — from Finland/Austria/Denmark's mature infrastructure to reported "steep readiness gaps" in Germany and unresolved regional fragmentation in Italy. A go-to-market plan built around a single assumed EU-wide adoption curve is likely to be wrong; country-by-country diligence is required before committing resources.
6. **Regulatory and legal-designation uncertainty persists in most countries.** As of the underlying research, formal HDAB/NCPeH designations remain unconfirmed or only provisional in several countries covered here (Germany, France, Estonia, Netherlands, Denmark, Sweden, Italy, Spain), and even Finland's presumed HDAB candidate (Findata) is explicitly described by its own materials as not yet formally decided. Selling into a still-forming governance structure means the buyer/decision-maker may itself be unsettled.

## Concrete next steps and watch-list items

- **Track the Commission's Annex II "common specifications" implementing act**, due by statute on 26 March 2027 (Article 36). This is the single highest-leverage date to watch — it is the legal trigger that converts today's draft/trial-use technical material into a binding target. See [02-implementation-timeline.md](02-implementation-timeline.md).
- **Track HL7 Europe's FHIR Implementation Guide releases** (Base, Core, Extensions, and the Patient Summary and Medication Prescription and Dispense IGs) for progression beyond "Standard for Trial Use" status, and monitor the joint **IHE Europe/HL7 Europe "EURIDICE"** initiative for new joint specifications — these are the leading technical candidates likely to inform the eventual common specifications.
- **Watch for a CEN/CENELEC standardization request/mandate explicitly tied to Regulation (EU) 2025/327.** The underlying research found no such mandate confirmed by name (only CEN/TC 251's general health-informatics remit) — its appearance (or continued absence) is a signal of how the formal European standardization system is being brought into the EHDS process.
- **Monitor the EU database for EHR system registration** (per Article 38) once it is operational — this is expected to be the authoritative public list of which EHR systems have completed conformity self-certification, and can double as market-intelligence on which vendors are actually compliant, country by country.
- **Track national implementing legislation directly** rather than relying on secondary commentary: Finland's STM consultation and Secondary Use Act reform; Germany's GeDIG passage; the Netherlands' Wgis/GDA bill following its July 2026 consultation close; France's expected 2026 transposition bill. Several of these were still pending as of the underlying research and their outcomes will determine the actual national buyer and procurement route in each country.
- **Watch Black Book Research's ongoing EHDS vendor-readiness series** (it appears to be the only entity currently publishing country-by-country EHDS vendor rankings) as a low-cost, if low-rigor, signal of competitive positioning — but corroborate any specific claim against vendors' own statements or official sources before relying on it.
- **Prioritize direct primary-source verification before committing capital.** Because this documentation set was built without working access to EUR-Lex and most primary government/vendor sites, a serious go/no-go decision should start with a direct, verified read of Regulation (EU) 2025/327's operative articles (especially Annex II, Article 36, and Article 105) at [eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng), plus direct outreach to Findata, THL, and Kela (for a Finland-first strategy) or the equivalent bodies in any other target country.
