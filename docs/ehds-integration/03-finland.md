# Finland

Finland is repeatedly described in the underlying research as one of the EU's most EHDS-ready member states, largely because its national digital-health backbone already delivers much of what EHDS requires EU-wide. This file covers Finland's infrastructure, authorities, vendor landscape, and what remains to be done. See the [overview](index.md) for methodology caveats — direct fetches of kanta.fi, thl.fi, stm.fi, findata.fi, valtioneuvosto.fi and related domains were blocked during research, so findings rely on search-engine synthesis of these sources.

## Kanta: the national backbone

**Kanta Services (Kanta-palvelut)** is Finland's mandatory national digital health/social-care data backbone, operated by **Kela** (the Social Insurance Institution of Finland) since 2015 (with the ePrescription service running earlier). It is "a centralised patient data repository for health, wellbeing and social welfare information, maintained by... Kela," with centralized national ePrescription and patient-data-repository services in place since 2015 providing "a solid basis for cross-border healthcare" ([Kanta.fi: EU Electronic Prescriptions and Health Records](https://www.kanta.fi/en/professionals/eu-electronic-prescriptions-and-health-records)). Kela is also responsible for developing Kanta so it complies with EHDS requirements ([Kanta/Kela presentation](https://yhteistyotilat.fi/wiki08/download/attachments/89634798/Kanta%20services%20presentation%20helsenorge%2020220812_final_v4.pdf)).

All public healthcare providers have been connected to the Patient Data Repository since 2014, and private providers must connect if they keep long-term electronic patient records; data entered in a local EHR is automatically transferred to the central repository ([Findata.fi](https://findata.fi/en/course/finnish-health-care-system/lessons/kanta-services-patient-data-repository/)).

Finland is cited as achieving an exceptionally high national interoperability rate (**98.9%**) via Kanta, attributed to mandatory participation, strong regulatory enforcement, and widespread HL7 FHIR adoption. A Black Book Research survey found European healthcare executives cite Finland (with Denmark) as a model for EHR interoperability and patient access, with roughly 90% of surveyed executives citing Kanta as an inspiration for nationwide interoperability ([Newswire coverage](https://www.newswire.com/news/european-health-it-leaders-look-to-denmark-and-finland-as-models-for-22588141); [Black Book Research, 2025 rankings](https://www.newswire.com/news/global-interoperability-gaps-revealed-in-2025-healthcare-connectivity-22560949)). In earlier European Commission (2013) and WHO (2016) benchmarking surveys, Finland scored ahead of the EU and global average on selected eHealth indicators ([gnius.esante.gouv.fr](https://gnius.esante.gouv.fr/en/international-e-health/finland)).

> ⚠️ **Unverified / conflicting sources**: No official, single quantitative EU-wide ranking (e.g., an eHealth Network or European Commission scoreboard) placing Finland relative to all 26 other member states was found; the comparisons above rely on a commercial survey (Black Book) and general commentary rather than an EU official interoperability index.

Kanta's roadmap ("tiekartta") for 2025–2029 (with later material referencing 2026–2030) explicitly includes "national implementation of the EHDS regulation" as a long-term development focus ([Kanta tiekartta 2026-2030](https://www.kanta.fi/documents/d/guest/kanta-tiekartta-2026-2030); [THL](https://thl.fi/-/kansallista-tietovarantoa-kartutetaan-kanta-palvelujen-painopisteet-on-vahvistettu)). 2026 development priorities include the Kanta medication list, information flow between social and healthcare services, storage of healthcare appointment data in Kanta, visibility of social-care client records in OmaKanta, and finalizing functional-capacity data and electronic medical certificates; Kanta's data content and interfaces are being gradually migrated to **HL7 FHIR**, with the first new FHIR content covering appointment data and cross-sector functional-capacity data ([Kela/STT Info press release](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/71758006/kanta-palvelut-vuonna-2026-kannan-tiedot-palvelevat-yha-paremmin-ammattilaisten-tyota)).

> ⚠️ **Unverified / conflicting sources**: Kanta's development funding is described as "significantly decreasing in 2026" due to public budget cuts, forcing strict prioritization of development work — including EHDS-related work ([Kela, 2026](https://www.kela.fi/ajankohtaista/kanta-palvelut-vuonna-2026-kannan-tiedot-palvelevat-yha-paremmin-ammattilaisten-tyota)). This is a real risk flag for the pace of EHDS-related technical work even though the policy commitment is strong.

## Government coordination: STM, THL, Kela

The **Ministry of Social Affairs and Health (STM)** is formally leading national EHDS implementation, having launched a coordination project, **"EHDS-asetuksen kansallisen toimeenpanon koordinaatio 2025–2027"** (Coordination of the national implementation of the EHDS Regulation 2025–2027), with a steering group and two working groups ([STM project page](https://stm.fi/en/project?tunnus=STM174:00/2025); [search synthesis of Kanta.fi/THL/tehdas.eu sources](https://www.iderha.org/news/implementing-ehds-national-level-news-finland)).

STM issued a public consultation ("lausuntopyyntö"), *"Terveystietojen jakamista ja hallintaa helpottava EU-asetus tuo muutoksia lainsäädäntöön"* (EU regulation facilitating the sharing and management of health data brings changes to legislation), and a related Valtioneuvosto press release states the regulation requires changes to the Patient Data Act and secondary legislation so patients' new rights and inter-member-state data exchange can be implemented within the regulation's transition periods. **EHDS implementation has been allocated €4.4 million** in STM's main budget category ([STM press release](https://stm.fi/-/lausuntopyynto-terveystietojen-jakamista-ja-hallintaa-helpottava-eu-asetus-tuo-muutoksia-lainsaadantoon); [Valtioneuvosto/STM press release](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1271139/eurooppalainen-terveysdata-avaruusasetus-ehds-aiheuttaa-muutoksia-potilastietojen-kasittelyyn-terveydenhuollossa)).

The consultation package covers supplementary regulation to (1) the law on electronic processing of social and health care customer data, (2) the law on electronic prescriptions, and (3) the law on market surveillance of certain products, with amended laws targeted to enter into force **1 January 2026** (search synthesis of STM/Edilex/Lausuntopalvelu consultation materials, [Lausuntopalvelu](https://www.lausuntopalvelu.fi/FI/Proposal/Participation?proposalId=3eb57f8f-0b54-4859-9a45-7a47ab676035)). The same package reportedly addresses patient-record archiving, potentially involving a National Archives (Kansallisarkisto) selection decision on permanent retention of public healthcare patient data ([search synthesis](https://www.edilex.fi/asiasanat/Potilasasiakirja)).

> ⚠️ **Unverified / conflicting sources**: No official Finnish document could be directly retrieved confirming the final, as-passed text or enactment date of these amended laws — only that a consultation targeted 1 January 2026 entry into force. This should be verified against stm.fi/finlex.fi directly.

**THL** (the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare) has been assigned by STM (for 2026) to help prepare EHDS implementation together with other government organizations and cooperation partners, "through data structures, operational models, and research," and states that "Kanta services are central in implementing the EHDS Regulation — in cooperation with all actors" ([THL](https://thl.fi/en/-/kanta-services-are-central-in-implementing-the-ehds-regulation-in-cooperation-with-all-actors); [THL blog, Finnish](https://blogi.thl.fi/kanta-palveluilla-on-keskeinen-rooli-ehds-asetuksen-toimeenpanossa-yhteistyossa-kaikkien-toimijoiden-kanssa/)). THL has also engaged clinical stakeholders directly: a Finnish Medical Journal (Lääkärilehti) interview quotes a THL chief physician stating "every doctor should be interested in EHDS" and that clinicians "have the opportunity to influence what kind of systems they will be using" ([Lääkärilehti](https://www.laakarilehti.fi/terveydenhuolto/sinulla-on-mahdollisuus-vaikuttaa-siihen-millaiset-jarjestelmat-sinulla-on-kaytossasi/)).

The functional division of labor appears to be: **STM** = policy/legal lead and EU-facing coordination; **THL** = technical/operational preparation support; **Kela** = operator of the underlying Kanta infrastructure carrying most primary-use obligations. STM also held a public webinar, *"Eurooppalainen terveysdata-avaruusasetus (EHDS) -webinaari: vaikutukset potilastietojärjestelmien toteutukseen"* (EHDS webinar: impacts on the implementation of patient information systems), on 7 November 2024 — directly aimed at HIS/EMR vendors, IT leads and provider organizations ([STM event page](https://stm.fi/tapahtumat/2024-11-07/eurooppalainen-terveysdata-avaruusasetus-ehds-webinaari-vaikutukset-potilastietojarjestelmien-toteutukseen)).

## MyHealth@EU: Finland is already a live participant

Finland already participates in MyHealth@EU/eHDSI, and in some respects is a pioneer:

- Finland is one of **25 EU countries** currently offering MyHealth@EU electronic cross-border health services ([European Commission](https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/digital-health-and-care/electronic-cross-border-health-services_en)).
- The **cross-border ePrescription (CBeP)** service launched in January 2019, initially letting Finnish patients fill Finnish ePrescriptions at Estonian pharmacies; by June 2020, Estonian ePrescriptions became dispensable in Finnish pharmacies too, making Estonia and Finland **pioneering countries for bidirectional cross-border ePrescription use** ([EC MyHealth@EU documentation](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/d4e2343d-fa08-4df9-baeb-b01536ba042f_en)).
- **Patient Summaries** (allergies, current medication, past illnesses, surgeries) are exchanged with other MyHealth@EU countries, giving treating clinicians essential information about visiting patients in their own language ([EC documentation](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/d4e2343d-fa08-4df9-baeb-b01536ba042f_en)).
- Finland is developing **FLaReS**, a Laboratory Result Report Service, for cross-border exchange of laboratory data ahead of the EHDS mandate — benefiting both traveling Finnish citizens and visitors seeking care in Finland ([EU4Health/MyHealth@EU project coverage](https://www.openaccessgovernment.org/new-eu4health-projects-push-digital-health-forward-across-europe/202153/)).
- **Kela's Contact Point for Cross-Border Health Care** has operated since 2014 (yhteyspiste@kela.fi, responds within 10 working days), and "Kela produces the eHDSI National Contact Point for eHealth" ([Kela: Contact Point](https://www.kela.fi/contact-point); [gnius.esante.gouv.fr](https://gnius.esante.gouv.fr/en/international-e-health/finland)).
- MyHealth@EU relies on **TESTA**, the secure European data-communication network (supported under the Digital Europe Programme) for exchanging sensitive information between public authorities ([interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/egovernment/solution/testa/news/secure-and-efficient-use-health-data)).

> ⚠️ **Unverified / conflicting sources**: No source explicitly confirms that the EHDS Regulation's formal "National Contact Point for Digital Health" role has been legally (re-)designated to Kela specifically under EHDS (as opposed to Kela's pre-existing eHDSI-era operational role) — continuity seems highly likely but was not found as an explicit post-March-2025 legal designation announcement.

## Findata: the likely secondary-use authority (HDAB)

**Findata** (the Finnish Social and Health Data Permit Authority), established in 2019 under the **Act on the Secondary Use of Health and Social Data** (the "Secondary Use Act"), is consistently described as the "closest equivalent" to — and de facto candidate for — Finland's EHDS Health Data Access Body (HDAB): "Findata serves as Finland's data permit authority and fulfills the role of the Health Data Access Body (HDAB) under the EHDS Regulation, operating as a 'one-stop shop' for data access" ([Findata.fi EHDS page](https://findata.fi/en/services-and-instructions/ehds/)). Findata's own page, however, qualifies this: **"No decisions have yet been made regarding national implementation, and national regulation does not yet include all the requirements set by EHDS"** ([Findata.fi](https://findata.fi/en/services-and-instructions/ehds/)) — so the HDAB role is anticipated/de facto rather than finally, formally confirmed.

Findata's establishment in 2019 made Finland "one of the first EU countries with a dedicated health data permit authority" ([Findata "About Findata"](https://findata.fi/en/)). Its functions — granting data permits, collecting and pseudonymizing data, setting requirements for secure processing environments — map directly onto the EHDS Regulation's HDAB functions ([Findata.fi](https://findata.fi/en/about-findata/your-data-rights/)). Findata is already participating in the EU's "Community of Practice," a collaborative network of national authorities (established January 2024) supporting secondary use of health data as part of EHDS implementation ([Findata.fi](https://findata.fi/en/faq/what-are-the-secondary-use-purposes-under-the-ehds-regulation/)).

Findata is building the secondary-use technical infrastructure through **FinHITS** ("Strengthening Finnish Health Data ICT for Secondary Use"), a four-year EU-co-funded project (2023–2027) explicitly intended to "prepare Finland for the requirements of the European Health Data Space (EHDS), such as cross-border infrastructure." FinHITS work includes Findata's e-service, the national data catalogue, data-quality tooling, and connecting Findata's e-service and national dataset catalogue to **HealthData@EU**; a new e-service to streamline data-permit applications is planned for launch in **autumn 2026** ([Findata: FinHITS project page](https://findata.fi/en/finhits/); [Spinverse](https://www.spinverse.com/references/finhits-strengthen-finnish-health-data-project-32)). Finland already has **ten EHDS-compliant secure data processing environments**, with approximately **5,000 users** and about **1,100 ongoing research projects** using Findata-permitted secondary-use data ([Findata/FinHITS reporting](https://findata.fi/en/news/findata-developes-finnish-secondary-use-services-in-the-finhits-project/)).

The likely functional split is: **Kela** = National Contact Point for Digital Health / primary-use cross-border exchange (continuing its pre-EHDS eHDSI role); **Findata** = Health Data Access Body / secondary-use national access point (continuing and expanding its Secondary Use Act role) — mirroring the EHDS Regulation's own primary-use/secondary-use structural distinction.

> ⚠️ **Unverified / conflicting sources**: No source confirms a final legal/statutory act formally designating Findata as Finland's EHDS HDAB. Treat this as "highly likely but not confirmed as formally finalized" based on available sources.

Finland's 2019 Secondary Use Act is undergoing a reform process to bring it fully into line with EHDS requirements; sources explicitly describe the Act and Kanta together as providing "a solid foundation for the European health data era" ([Kanta.fi](https://www.kanta.fi/en/professionals/priorities-of-the-kanta-services); [Dittmar & Indrenius Attorneys](https://www.dittmar.fi/news/the-reform-of-the-finnish-act-on-the-secondary-use-of-social-and-health-data/) — title/topic confirmed via search, full text not retrievable).

## Finnish HIS/EMR vendors and systems

Finland's HIS/EMR market is now consolidated mainly around **three vendors** serving the country's 21 wellbeing services counties (hyvinvointialueet), plus a separate Epic-based system in Helsinki-Uusimaa:

- **TietoEvry (Lifecare)** — replaced the older Effica system as a leading customer/patient information system.
- **CGI** — legacy Pegasos/Uranus systems, now consolidating into the current-generation **OMNI360** product ("a Finnish patient information system for people") ([CGI Finland](https://www.cgi.com/fi/fi/tuoteratkaisut/omni360-asiakas-ja-potilastietojarjestelma)).
- **Mediconsult**.

Older systems Effica, Pegasos and Uranus are described as being consolidated/retired ("Effica, Pegasos ja Uranus jäävät historiaan") ([Mediuutiset/Kauppalehti](https://www.mediuutiset.fi/uutiset/effica-pegasos-ja-uranus-jaavat-historiaan/60666593-f4d8-3476-9d8f-d59970718586)). Recent regional procurements: **Pohjanmaa** wellbeing region selected TietoEvry's Lifecare; **Pirkanmaa** and **Pohjois-Savo** selected CGI's OMNI360; **East Uusimaa** and **West Uusimaa** processes involved bids from CGI, Mediconsult and TietoEvry (TietoEvry won at least East Uusimaa); **Central Finland (Keski-Suomi)** is moving toward one shared patient information system ([Pohjanmaan hyvinvointialue](https://pohjanmaanhyvinvointi.fi/tietoevry-toimittaa-uuden-asiakas-ja-potilastietojarjestelman/); [Pirha.fi](https://www.pirha.fi/w/cgi-n-jarjestelma-valittu-uudeksi-potilastietojarjestelmaksi-pirkanmaalle); [Itä-Uudenmaan hyvinvointialue](https://itauusimaa.fi/2024/12/18/ita-uudenmaan-hyvinvointialue-valitsi-tietoevryn-asiakas-ja-potilastietojarjestelman-toimittajakseen/); [Länsi-Uudenmaan hyvinvointialue](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70771534/lansi-uudenmaan-hyvinvointialue-valitsi-lifecare-kokonaisuuden-asiakas-ja-potilastietojarjestelmakseen); [Keski-Suomen hyvinvointialue](https://www.hyvaks.fi/uutiset/keski-suomen-hyvinvointialueelle-tulossa-yksi-yhteinen-potilastietojarjestelma)).

**Apotti** is a separate, joint EHR project of the City of Helsinki, Vantaa, Kirkkonummi and Kauniainen together with the Hospital District of Helsinki and Uusimaa (HUS); HUS implemented an **Epic**-based EHR (branded Apotti) in four phases during 2018–2021. It combines a core Epic entity plus supplementary systems/integrations and is described as enabling joint data use and open interfaces in real time "within the boundaries of legislation" ([Apotti ecosystem](https://www.apotti.fi/en/the-apotti-ecosystem/); [PMC: Implementing a New EHR System in a University Hospital](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9222436/)). FHIR is recommended as the interface technology for Apotti integrations, though at least one internal interoperability limitation is documented: HUS specialized medical care cannot search a patient's primary-care information within Apotti due to current system limitations ([grokipedia.com, based on Apotti technical literature](https://grokipedia.com/page/Apotti)).

### What Finnish vendors and hospitals concretely need to do

- **No vendor** (TietoEvry, CGI, Mediconsult, or the Apotti alliance/HUS) was found in this research to have published a public EHDS-specific compliance roadmap, EEHRxF adoption plan, or gateway/interoperability project. This is treated as a genuine information gap, not confirmation of inactivity.
- The practical channel through which Finnish HIS vendors will track EHDS-driven interface changes is likely **Kanta.fi's system-developer status pages** (e.g., "Potilastietovarannon tilanne," the Patient Data Repository status page for system developers) ([Kanta.fi](https://www.kanta.fi/jarjestelmakehittajat/pta-tilanne)).
- With HIS provision concentrated in essentially three vendors plus Apotti/Epic, EEHRxF/FHIR-based interface requirements will functionally need to be implemented by a small number of vendor engineering teams plus Kela's Kanta interfaces — a narrower vendor-coordination problem than in more fragmented EU markets, potentially working in Finland's favor for meeting deadlines. However, the recent wave of regional system replacements (2023–2025) suggests significant vendor engineering bandwidth is currently absorbed by migration projects rather than free for EHDS-specific work.
- Concretely, by the 2029/2031 milestones (see [02-implementation-timeline.md](02-implementation-timeline.md)), Finnish hospitals and vendors will need Kanta/HIS interfaces that support EEHRxF-aligned FHIR exchange for patient summaries and ePrescriptions (2029) and imaging/lab/discharge reports (2031), plus mechanisms to flag/respect the EHDS secondary-use opt-out and route data to Findata's secure processing environments.

> ⚠️ **Unverified / conflicting sources**: Direct fetches of kanta.fi, apotti.fi, cgi.com and tietoevry.com were blocked during research, so it could not be confirmed whether Apotti/Epic has any EHDS- or EEHRxF-specific integration project underway beyond general Kanta/FHIR integration.
